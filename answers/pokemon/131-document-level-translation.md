# You cannot translate a Pokédex one entry at a time

Hand a translator a single line — *"It evolves at level 16."* — and ask for it in another region's
language.

**Evolves into what?** The line does not say. The line before it said. And that line has been given
to a different translator, in a different room, who will never speak to this one.

That is sentence-by-sentence translation. It is the default because that is how the practice
material is filed, and it is **structurally unable** to get certain things right — not because it
is careless, but because the information it needs is on another page.

## The things that live on another page 📄

**♀♂ The ones where the symbol is the species.** Nothing in Pokémon makes this sharper than
**Nidoran**. There are two of them. ♀ becomes **Nidorina**, then **Nidoqueen**. ♂ becomes
**Nidorino**, then **Nidoking**. Two species, two lines, two completely different Pokémon.

```
   entry 29:  "Nidoran♀ — it has small barbs that secrete a powerful poison."
   entry 30:  "It evolves from the previous one at level 16."
                ▲
        which one? the entry does not say. the ♀ was one line up.
        get it wrong and you have written Nidorino into the Nidoqueen line.
```

English will happily say "it". Many languages **cannot** — they must mark it, and marking it
requires knowing something the sentence does not contain. A translator working one line at a time
does not choose from evidence. It chooses from habit, and it is right about half the time.

**Meowstic** is the same trap with a subtler edge: the male and the female genuinely differ in
what they can do. Blur them and the entry is not clumsy, it is **wrong**.

**🎩 How formally you address people.** A **Gym Leader** greeting a challenger and a rival
sneering at one are not the same register — and whichever you choose, you choose it **once, for
the whole book.** A Pokédex that slips between courtly and casual between entries reads as broken,
and in some regions it reads as rude.

**🏷️ Calling the same thing the same thing.** A **Poké Ball** on page one must still be a Poké
Ball on page four hundred. Each entry alone is perfectly fine with a fresh synonym. The *book* is
not.

**🔗 Words that point backwards.** "However." "Then." "Meanwhile." Each is a claim about something
that already happened.

**📏 And the numbers.** **Wailord** is 14.5 metres. Convert that one way in the height field and
another in the prose and the reader will never trust either again.

## Ways to give the translator the page 📚

| How | What it costs |
| --- | --- |
| 📎 Hand over the previous few entries alongside the one being translated | simple, effective; the context thins as the book grows |
| 📖 Hand over the **whole Pokédex** at once | far and away the most consistent — but it may merge two entries into one, and now nothing lines up |
| 🔁 Translate entry by entry, then **read the whole book back** and fix the clashes | keeps every line aligned, catches the terminology |
| 📓 Keep a **decision log** — every choice made, enforced from then on | dull, deterministic, and the only one that truly guarantees consistency |

## Why nobody noticed this for years 📉

Here is the awkward part, and it is the reason the whole problem was ignored.

⚠️ **Fix every single ♀/♂ mix-up in the entire Pokédex, and the season score barely moves.**

Most entries do not depend on the previous page. They dominate the average. So a Pokédex that is
*obviously* better to anybody actually reading it registers as a rounding error against a Pokédex
that gets Nidoqueen and Nidoking backwards.

📌 So test the thing you fixed, directly. **Write two versions of the entry that differ by exactly
one symbol** — ♀ against ♂, nothing else — and ask which one the translator prefers. Now you are
measuring the capability itself, and it cannot drown in the mean.

Worth counting too: how often the same item keeps the same name through the book, and whether the
register flips somewhere around entry two hundred.

## Practical notes 🔧

* 📐 **Line the pages up properly first.** Feed a translator context from the wrong entries and you
  have made things worse than no context at all.
* 🏷️ **Tell it what kind of book this is** — who it is for, how formal — rather than letting it
  guess from the first line and commit for four hundred pages.
* 🌫️ **Watch it drift.** Hand over a very long book and quality sags in the middle, exactly as it
  does with any long text (question 046). Entry four hundred will not sound like entry four.
* 📋 **Keep the glossary and enforce it** (question 133). Having the page in view makes consistency
  *likely*. Only the list makes it certain.
