# Nobody can sell you an **Eevee**. Everybody sells the stone

There is exactly one **Eevee** in **Red** and **Blue**. It is sitting in a building in **Celadon
City**, it is free, and when you have taken it there is no second one. The **Water Stone**, the
**Thunder Stone** and the **Fire Stone** are on a shelf four doors away in the **Celadon
Department Store** at 2,100 each, and anybody with pocket money can buy all three. The scarce
thing was given away. The cheap thing is what decides what you end up holding — and that is the
entire arrangement, stated in one city.

## The arithmetic of the asymmetry

**Eevee**'s **Base Stat Total** is 325: 55 HP, 55 Attack, 50 Defence, 45 Special Attack, 65
Special Defence, 55 Speed. Nothing outstanding anywhere. Every single one of its eight evolutions
totals **525** — the same 200 extra points, every time — and every one of them puts **130** into
exactly one place and leaves the rest ordinary. **Vaporeon** puts it in HP. **Jolteon** puts it in
Speed. **Flareon** puts it in Attack. **Espeon** in Special Attack, **Umbreon** in Special
Defence, **Leafeon** in Defence, **Glaceon** in Special Attack again, **Sylveon** in Special
Defence.

Two hundred points is what the stone buys. The 325 underneath it is what somebody else already
paid for, and no shop stocks it.

The list is ordered by what it costs, and it is *also* ordered by how much of your **Eevee** it
destroys. That is not a coincidence. The move that changes the most is the move that takes the
most away.

## The wrinkle that **Eevee** specifically introduces

You are not shaping something blank. Two of the eight routes depend on what is *already* there,
and if you have been careless with it the route is simply closed:

* **Sylveon** needs a **Fairy**-type move sitting in one of the four slots at the moment it levels
  up — **Charm** will do. Overwrite that slot while tidying up the moveset and the evolution
  silently stops happening. Nothing tells you why.
* **Espeon** and **Umbreon** are the same evolution split by the clock: high friendship by day
  gives you one, high friendship by night gives you the other. Train at the wrong hour and you get
  the other one, permanently.
* **Friendship goes backwards.** It drops every time **Eevee** faints, and it drops again if you
  reach for bitter medicine like an **Energy Powder** instead of a **Super Potion**. The training
  itself can undo the thing you were training towards, and the counter is invisible.
* **Keep some ordinary fights in the mix.** An **Eevee** levelled entirely on one Gym's Pokémon
  carries **Effort Values** in exactly the stats that Gym happened to hand out, and nothing
  anywhere else. Mixing the opponents is the cheapest insurance there is, and it costs one
  afternoon.

## What a stone actually touches

A stone does not adjust **Eevee**. It replaces it. Every stat moves at once, the typing changes,
the **Pokédex** number changes, and there is no menu anywhere that turns a **Flareon** back. The
only thing that reaches into that decision at all is an **Everstone** in its hand, which stops the
change from happening rather than steering it.

And the catalogue is not fixed. In **Kanto** there were three stones and three answers. **Johto**
added **Espeon** and **Umbreon** with no stone at all. **Sinnoh** added **Leafeon** and
**Glaceon** and made you stand in a particular place to get them. **Kalos** added **Sylveon**. The
**Eevee** never changed. Four new ways to spend it arrived over twenty years, for a creature that
was already finished.

## The direction most Trainers forget

Evolving it is not the only thing to do with it. **Leaving it in the party and letting it carry
something weaker is frequently better.** Put a **Lucky Egg** on the front and an **Exp. Share** on
the one you actually intend to use, and the strong Pokémon does the fighting while the small one
collects what the fights were worth. You end up with something you can take anywhere, trained on
work it could never have done itself. The **Vaporeon** costs you an afternoon. The thing it raised
is what you carry.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

That you start at the top of the list and can say what would have to fail before you reach for a
stone. Good answers name the **Fairy**-type move as a precondition rather than a detail, and the
ordinary fights as the insurance. The best notice that shaping a Pokémon somebody else already
shaped is a different job from raising one from an Egg, and that a strong **Vaporeon** is often
worth more as the thing standing behind an **Exp. Share** than as the thing on the field.

## Where this stands, September 2026

**Eevee**'s 325, the 525 shared by all eight evolutions, the 130 each one puts in one stat, the
three stones at 2,100 in the **Celadon Department Store**, the day-and-night split between
**Espeon** and **Umbreon**, the **Fairy**-type requirement for **Sylveon**, the **Everstone** and
the absence of reverse evolution are all from the games and do not rot. Which shop stocks which
stone, and how many evolutions exist at all, is something each release states about itself and has
changed four times already. Check the shelf you are actually standing at.
