# Greninja changes type every single turn, and the referee cannot cope

You know Protean. In the generations where it fired on every move, Greninja **became the type
of whatever it had just used**. Water Shuriken and it is Water. Ice Beam and it is Ice. Dark
Pulse and it is Dark. Three turns, three Greninjas.

Nothing is broken. That is the ability working exactly as designed, and a Greninja running it
is not a confused Pokémon — it is a very good one.

Now watch the paperwork fall apart around it.

```
  1. 🗃️ THE FILING CLERK   ► "Type?" — half Water, half Dark, 55% sure.
                              Files it under "malformed" and throws it out.
                              ► Greninja is now absent from the entire archive.

  2. 🏪 CELADON TM COUNTER ► stocked properly for Water, barely for Dark.
                              Half the turn is one clean press, half is
                              spelled out letter by letter.

  3. 🧠 THE POKÉDEX        ► trained on Pokémon that pick a type and keep it.
                              Has genuinely never seen a mid-battle switch.

  4. 🗣️ THE COMMENTARY     ► calls the whole match as if it were a Water battle,
                              or flips to Dark at turn two and never flips back.

  5. 📋 BATTLE TOWER LOGS  ► not one entry in the official record is a Pokémon
                              that did this. Nothing to compare against.
```

## Stage one is the real damage 🗑️

The clerk is the disaster, because the clerk is invisible. It does not report *"discarded 40%
of Kalos"* — it reports nothing at all, and years later somebody asks why the Pokédex is bad at
Greninja and the answer is that **Greninja was never in the book.**

Cinderace gets filed under "malformed" for the same reason — Libero is the same mechanic on a
different body, so this is not one strange Pokémon, it is a family of them.

Kecleon has the same problem from the other side. Color Change makes it become the type of
whatever just hit it, so every log of a Kecleon battle contradicts every other log, and the
clerk bins the lot as inconsistent.

## Why "just pick a type" is the wrong instruction 🚫

Because there is nothing to fix. Protean Greninja is not a Water-type behaving badly. Both
types are correct, in sequence, on purpose, and by rule — Protean has switch conditions and
follows them. A referee who insists on one stamp per Pokémon is not tidying up a mess. **They
are refusing to referee this battle.**

## What actually works 🛠️

* 🔖 **Stamp per turn, not per battle.** If you must label it, label each move as it happens —
  and when you are unsure, **keep the log** instead of shredding it. An uncertain record beats a
  missing one every time.
* 🧑‍🎓 **Drill the Pokédex on real switching battles.** Being fluent in Water and fluent in Dark
  separately does not make you ready for a Pokémon that alternates. That has to be practised
  directly, and practising it works.
* 🏗️ **Manufacture practice matches, but respect the rules.** You can script switching battles
  from ordinary ones — but Protean fires on the *move*, at the *moment* it is used. Invent
  switch points that could never happen and you are drilling your Pokédex on battles that do
  not exist.
* 🗣️ **Answer in the register you were asked in.** A Trainer who calls out orders half in one
  language and half in another and gets a reply in flawless, formal one-language prose has been
  answered by something that did not listen.
* ⌨️ **It will not even be written in the right alphabet.** Most Trainers who battle like this
  write their notes entirely in Latin letters no matter which language the words came from — so
  the switch is real but hidden, and a clerk drilled on proper script sees only nonsense.
* 🔊 **Cries are worse than notes.** A written log at least sits still. A Pokémon's *cry* has to
  be recognised on the fly, and most listeners are told which region to expect **before** the
  battle starts and cannot change their mind halfway through.

📌 Protean is not a bug in Greninja. Every failure in that list is a failure of the paperwork
around it, and the first and worst one is a clerk quietly throwing the evidence away.
