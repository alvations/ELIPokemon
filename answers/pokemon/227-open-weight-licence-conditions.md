# A traded Pokémon keeps somebody else's name on it forever

Open the summary of a Pokémon you were given and there it is, on the same page as the stats and
never on the sticker: the **Original Trainer**'s name, and an **ID No.** that is not yours. It
does not wear off. It does not change when it evolves. And what it *does* to you depends entirely
on which arrangement the trade was made under — because that changed between two releases of the
same line, twelve months apart.

## The first arrangement: it obeys, and it keeps the credit

Under the first set of terms the Pokémon is simply yours to use. Train it, evolve it, breed from
it, put it in any team. One thing only is fixed: **the name and the ID No. stay on the summary**,
and the **Name Rater** will refuse to touch it. "That's not your Pokémon," he says, and that is
the end of the conversation.

That is a credit line and nothing else. You cannot remove it and almost nobody notices it. For
practically every Trainer it is no restriction at all.

## The second arrangement: it obeys up to a point

The newer terms are a different document. The rights are just as broad — battle it, raise it,
evolve it, trade it on — and then there is a gate.

**An outsider Pokémon obeys you only up to the level your badges cover.** Take **Misty**'s
**Cascade Badge** at the **Cerulean Gym** and it will follow orders to level 30. Past that it
stops. It turns away. It loafs around. It falls asleep mid-battle. It uses a move you did not
choose. The Pokémon has not changed and its power has not changed — **what changed is how far
along you are**, and the gate is set against your progress, not against its.

And getting the full thing can require a second party outright. **Machoke** will not become
**Machamp** in your hands no matter how many levels you pour into it; it becomes **Machamp** when
it is traded, and not before. The same is true of **Graveler** into **Golem**, **Haunter** into
**Gengar**, **Kadabra** into **Alakazam**, of **Scyther** holding a **Metal Coat**, of **Seadra**
holding a **Dragon Scale**, of **Poliwhirl** holding a **King's Rock**. The deal is not a fee
afterwards. It is the step itself, and nothing happens until it is done.

## The part that generalises

**The mark belongs to the Pokémon, not to the Trainer who bred it.** Two Pokémon from the same
breeder, a year apart, can come under completely different arrangements, and a Trainer who checked
the first one and assumed the second was the same will find out at level 31, in the middle of
**Victory Road**, with no move being used.

Two more things that are true of every gift Pokémon and get skipped:

* **Being allowed to use it is not the same as it being yours.** The **Name Rater** draws that
  line out loud. So does the obedience check.
* **You cannot rebuild it.** You have the Pokémon. You do not have its parents, you do not have
  the **Egg Group** it came from, and you cannot produce another one like it from first
  principles.

## What to actually do

* **Keep the summary screen, not the memory of it.** The **Original Trainer** and the **ID No.**
  are on the Pokémon; go and look at them rather than recalling what the last one said.
* **Re-check on every new gift, not on every new breeder.** The arrangement rides with the
  Pokémon.
* **Write down which badge you are relying on, and when.** That note is what makes the next check
  quick.
* **Check what it is holding** if you are counting on the **Everstone**.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

That you read the summary screen rather than the box it came in, and that you tell the difference
between a name you cannot remove and a gate that stops the battle. Good answers notice the gate is
set by your badges rather than by the Pokémon. The best notice that the trade is the step itself,
not a bill that arrives afterwards.

## Where this stands, September 2026

The obedience rule, the **Original Trainer** mark and the trade evolutions are from the games and
do not rot. Which release came under which arrangement is something each one stated about itself,
and one of them could not be checked at first hand from here at all. Before you rely on a gift,
open its summary — that Pokémon's, not the last one's. That instruction has no expiry date.
