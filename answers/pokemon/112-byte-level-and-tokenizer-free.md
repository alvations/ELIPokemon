# Close the TM counter. Everybody spells everything out.

The TM shelf decided, years ago and permanently, whose orders are one button and whose are
twelve. It decided it by looking at who was shopping at the time.

So: burn the shelf. No bundled moves for anyone. Every Trainer, every region, spells every order
out one letter at a time.

## What that buys you 🎁

**⚖️ Perfect fairness, by construction.** Nobody's language is under-stocked, because nothing is
stocked. Kanto spells its orders out. Paldea spells its orders out. The counter has no opinion
about who matters.

**🩹 Nothing is ever unreadable.** A new region turns up with a script nobody has ever seen —
Unown shapes, Braille bumps, a species named with a symbol in it — and it goes through like
anything else. There is no such thing as a mark the counter does not stock.

**🧱 It survives a mess.** This is the underrated one.

```
   📦 THE BUNDLED WAY              🔤 THE LETTER-BY-LETTER WAY
   ─────────────────              ──────────────────────────
   "Thunderbolt"  ► 1 press       T-h-u-n-d-e-r-b-o-l-t ► 11 steps
   "Thunderblot"  ► bundle
      missed. Falls apart into    T-h-u-n-d-e-r-b-l-o-t ► 11 steps
      six wrong pieces.              and it barely notices
   "ThUNDERbolt"  ► different
      bundle entirely.            Same again. Nothing special happened.
```

A Trainer yelling with a hoarse voice, a smudged notebook, half of one region's words inside
another region's sentence — the letter-by-letter counter shrugs at all of it. The bundle-based
one shatters.

**🔍 It can see inside a word.** Ask a bundled Trainer how many letters are in "Farfetch'd" and
it genuinely does not know — it never saw the letters, only the bundle. The letter-by-letter one
counts them.

And the awkward names are where the difference shows. **Farfetch'd** has an apostrophe. **Mr.
Mime** has a full stop and a space. **Porygon-Z** has a hyphen. **Jangmo-o** has both. Nidoran
comes in two species distinguished by a symbol most keyboards cannot type. The bundle counter
has a shelf slot for exactly the spellings it happened to see; everything else becomes rubble.
The letter rack simply reads them, marks and all.

Smeargle is the mascot for the whole approach: a Pokémon whose entire method is Sketch —
copying whatever it is shown, one move at a time — instead of turning up with four fixed
bundles chosen for it in advance.

## What it costs 💸

**⏳ Every order takes four times as many turns.** That is the whole bill, and it is a big one.

**🎒 The bag holds fewer ideas.** You were promised an enormous bag; it holds letters now, so
your Pokédex entry is four times longer for the same content. The fairness is real, but part of
the win gets eaten right back.

**🧠 The start of the order is a long way from the end.** By the time your Pokémon reaches the
final letter of a long instruction, the first letter is eleven turns behind it. Bundles kept the
whole idea in view at once.

## The middle path everybody is actually building 🛤️

Do not choose. **Let the Pokémon bundle on the fly.**

Common runs it has seen a thousand times — "Thunder", "Pokémon Center" — it takes in one gulp.
Strange runs it has never seen — an unfamiliar name, a smudge, a mark from the Sealed Chamber —
it slows down and takes letter by letter. Effort goes where the difficulty is instead of where
the shopping was busy a decade ago.

📌 So: the shelf is not gone, but it stopped being decided in advance by whoever happened to be
in Celadon that year. **The letter rack is the honest design. The bundles are the cheap one.**
Take letters when your Trainers are noisy, multi-script, or from a region nobody stocked for —
and take bundles when you simply cannot afford the turns.
