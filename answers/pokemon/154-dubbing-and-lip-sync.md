# The mouth is already moving and you cannot change it

Subtitles have to fit a **box** (question 144). A dub has to fit a **mouth**, and the mouth was
animated months ago in another country and is not going to wait for you.

Three constraints, all at once, none of them negotiable.

## What has to line up 🎬

**1. ⏱️ It must take exactly as long.** Start when the mouth starts, stop when it stops. Not
roughly — a line that runs over crashes into whoever speaks next.

**2. 👄 The mouth closes, so the sound must close it.** This is the one everybody notices without
knowing why. To make a **p**, **b** or **m** the lips have to shut completely. If the animation
shows a closed mouth and your line has an open vowel there, every single viewer feels that
something is wrong. **Pikachu** and **Bulbasaur** both begin by shutting the mouth; **Charizard**
and **Onix** do not. Put an Onix where a Bulbasaur was and it looks dubbed.

**3. 👋 The gestures are already timed.** The nod, the shrug, the moment the **Poké Ball** leaves
the hand, the beat where **Meowth** lands the joke. Put your emphatic word somewhere else and the
character is now emphasising a shrug.

```
   original :  |  It's  |  im-POS-sible  |  !  |   ← closes on "p", stress in the middle
                  ▲          ▲              ▲
   bad dub  :  |    that simply cannot happen    |  ← too long, nothing closes, flat
   good dub :  |  But  |  that's  MAD-ness  | !  |  ← same length, "m" shuts the mouth,
                                                     same beat
```

📌 And notice: **the good line is not the most accurate translation.** Dubbing deliberately trades
precision for fit, and a translator who refuses to make that trade hands back lines nobody can use.

## Doing it automatically 🤖

An automatic dub is question 153 with a stopwatch bolted on:

```
   the audio ─► write it down WITH TIMINGS ─► translate to a length ─► speak it to a duration
                        │                              │                        │
                 who is speaking            put the pauses where          stretch, pad,
                 (Meowth? Nurse Joy?)       the original paused           or re-pace
```

The levers, from least to most damaging:

* **✂️ Write it to length in the first place** (question 144) — produce several versions and keep
  the one that fits. Cheapest, and it costs almost nothing.
* **🤫 Move the silences, not the words.** Nearly free.
* **🐇 Speak faster or slower.** A little is inaudible. A lot sounds either panicked or half
  asleep, and **this is precisely how a bad automatic dub gives itself away.**
* **🩹 Stretch the finished audio.** Audible almost immediately. Last resort.

## What a machine still cannot do 🎭

* **🎙️ The performance.** A voice actor makes *choices* — irony, a hesitation, something withheld.
  A machine gives you the average reading of the line. This is the real gap, and it is closing far
  more slowly than the timing problem is.
* **🔁 Staying the same character across a whole series** — including when a **Charmander** becomes
  a **Charmeleon** and then a **Charizard**, and the voice has to grow with it while still being
  recognisably the same Pokémon.
* **🎵 The songs.** **Team Rocket's motto** is the case study: it rhymes, it scans, it has a rhythm
  two characters trade back and forth. Every region **re-writes** it — new rhymes, new jokes, same
  swagger. ⚠️ That is not translation. That is verse composition to a brief, and question 150's
  point about names applies double.
* **🌏 References that have to be swapped**, not rendered (question 150).

## Judging it 🏅

⚠️ The usual translation scores are close to worthless here, because **the constraint they cannot
see is the entire product.** A perfect COMET score on a line that runs two seconds long is a
perfect score on something that will never air.

* ⏲️ **How many lines fit the time**, within tolerance.
* 👄 **How well the mouths agree**, automatically for the whole episode and by eye for the close-ups.
* 📊 **The quality of the lines that fitted** — so you can see what the constraint cost you.
* 👀 **And whether viewers can tell.** That remains the only measure that captures the thing you
  were actually trying to achieve.
