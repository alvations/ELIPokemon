# The Ruins of Alph are full of writing nobody can read fast enough

There is a research station outside **Violet City** whose entire job is the carvings on those walls.
Not a shortage of material — the walls are covered. A shortage of **anyone with time to work
through them**, and a script whose reading is still argued over.

That is language documentation, and it is a different situation from question 130 rather than a
smaller one.

## There may be nothing to work from at all 🕳️

Question 130 assumed you had *something*: monolingual text, a related relative, a Ditto to breed
with. Here you may have **none of that.**

What actually exists is usually:

* 📼 field recordings, some of them decades old, on media that is deteriorating;
* 📓 researchers' notebooks, transcribed inconsistently by different hands;
* 📖 a dictionary, and if you are lucky a sketch of the grammar;
* 📜 one long religious text, frequently the largest single thing written down.

⚠️ And **the Egg Group trick may simply not be available.** Some species have no relatives — and
some have relatives that are **equally undocumented**. A **Relicanth** that has not changed in a
hundred million years has nobody left to breed with.

## What genuinely helps 🛠️

The honest list is unglamorous, and it is mostly **tools for the people doing the work** rather
than products for anybody else:

* **🎙️ Something that takes a first pass at the recordings.** This is the bottleneck and almost
  nothing else is. Working through an hour of tape by hand can take **forty**. A rough automatic
  transcript that a person then corrects changes what a two-person team can get through in a year.
  📌 If you build one thing, build this.
* **⌨️ A way to type the script at all.** The Ruins' characters are useless to anybody if there is
  no keyboard for them. That is a plain technical problem with a plain technical fix.
* **📔 Spellcheckers and dictionaries** — the things speakers actually open daily, unlike almost
  everything a research project produces.
* **🗄️ Somewhere for it to live afterwards.** Stable formats, proper labelling, and hosting that
  outlasts whoever paid for the project.
* **🔎 And search over what already exists**, so somebody can find *how my grandmother said this*.

## What mostly does not 🚫

Building a general translator from a few thousand carvings produces something that neither
speakers nor learners can rely on — ⚠️ **and its existence gets used as a reason not to fund the
people.**

📌 Ask what was actually wanted. It is very rarely a talking Pokédex.

## The spelling is not settled, and that is not a preprocessing step 🔠

The **Unown** alphabet has competing readings. Several groups have written the same wall down
different ways, and the differences track real disagreements about who these carvings belong to.

⚠️ **Picking one for your dataset is taking a side.** Record which system each text uses. Support
converting between them **where the community has defined how**. Never quietly normalise — it looks
like tidying and it is a ruling.

## And here the Pokémon framing stops 🤝

This part is about people, so it should be said plainly.

This field has a documented history of **extractive research**: material collected from
communities, published for academic credit, and then unavailable to the people it came from. The
response — the CARE principles for Indigenous data governance, alongside FAIR — puts **collective
benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics** on the same footing as openness.

In practice that means: the community decides what is collected and what is public; speakers are
**paid**; the licence is agreed **in advance** and may restrict commercial use and model training;
whatever you produce goes back in a form they can actually use; and if the project ends when the
grant ends, **say that at the beginning.**

⚠️ **"We scraped what was online" is not a methodology here.** Much of what is online is sacred, is
restricted, or was put there without the speaker's consent in the first place.
