# There is a truck beside the S.S. Anne. There is no Mew under it.

The truck is real. You can reach it with **Surf** after the ship leaves, and you can use
**Strength** on it, and nothing happens — because nothing was ever there. The rumour was so
specific and so confident that a generation of Trainers spent hours on it.

What makes it a perfect lesson is the part people forget: **Mew was genuinely in Red and Blue**,
reachable by a sequence the developers never intended. The rumour was wrong about the truck and
right about the direction. That is exactly how rumours about unreleased things behave, and it is
why you sort claims instead of believing or dismissing them wholesale.

```

  ├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 2. CHECKABLE WITH   │ "It swept the Battle Tower." Meaningful only │
  │    WORK             │ with the format, the rules, the held items   │
  │                     │ and who it fought. Repeatable in principle;  │
  │                     │ almost never repeated.                       │

  │ 3. ONLY CHECKABLE   │ "Strongest in the game." "Four to two        │
```

## Where to look, in order

1. **The Pokédex entry and the summary screen.** Type, stats, **Ability**, **Nature**, **Held
   Item**, where it was met. Dull and final.
2. **The game's own rules.** The **Type Chart**. It has never once been wrong.
3. **What the company put on the box.** True, and chosen. Read it for what it does *not* say.
4. **Careful write-ups by people who played it.** Good for the things the box leaves out.
5. **Playground repetition.** Most of it is copied from other playgrounds. A claim that exists
   only here does not exist.
6. **Rumour.** Plan a route with it. Never rest a team on it.

## The specific traps

* **Family collapse.** "An Eeveelution handles that" — which one? Eight types, eight answers
  (question 210).
* **Name collisions.** Kantonian **Raichu** and Alolan **Raichu** are different Pokémon wearing
  one name (question 210).
* **A cleared route reported as a ceiling.** Sweeping every **Youngster** means the route ran
  out, not that the Pokémon did not (question 211).
* **Fighting the wrong opponent.** A boast measured against a level-12 **Onix** is not a boast.
* **Enormous ranges.** "Four to two hundred times" tells you the opponents were wildly
  different, and nothing about where yours sits.
* **Counting turns instead of counting badges.** A cheap Pokémon that needs three attempts and a
  **Revive** is not cheap.

And the twist worth keeping: **Missingno.** was a rumour that turned out to be real, would
genuinely duplicate the sixth item in your bag, and would genuinely corrupt your **Hall of Fame**
on the way out. True does not mean safe.

## The discipline that actually works

**Build your gauntlet before the next announcement.** Twenty to fifty opponents from the routes
you actually walk, scored a way you trust. It costs an afternoon and turns every new Pokémon from
an argument into a battle. Record the ID number, record what actually fought, record the items
(question 210). Then the only question a launch raises is when you get round to the rematch.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

That you ask what kind of claim it is before asking whether it is true, and that you know which
facts are free. The strongest answers say "I would run my gauntlet" and can describe it — how
many opponents, what counts as a win, what it costs.

## Where this stands, September 2026

This arc is its own worked example. The brief behind it named a "lunar" Pokémon that does not
exist — there is a **Luna** — treated "Astra" as one thing when two Trainers own one each, and
set Upstage's **Solar** beside OpenAI's **Sol** as though they were the same line. Every one of
those is an ordinary mistake made from second-hand material. Everything in questions 201–212 is a
September 2026 snapshot built from what was written down, not from battles fought, and it will
date faster than anything else in this dataset. Treat it as tier three, and go and read the
entry.
