# Instruction tuning is one week of obedience school

Your Pokémon comes out of the wild grass knowing **everything**. Every type matchup, every
terrain, every species. It is, in raw terms, already a monster.

Say "Thunderbolt" and it ignores you completely. It knows the move. It knows Gyarados is
Water/Flying and takes four times damage. It simply does not know that *you* are relevant.

Not out of defiance. It has genuinely never encountered the concept that *a human making noises*
is a thing that should change what it does. In the grass, sounds happen. You respond to the ones
that are threats. That's it.

## What the week actually teaches 🎓

```
   🗣️  TRAINER SAYS:    "Thunderbolt the Gyarados."
   ⚡  POKÉMON DOES:    Thunderbolts the Gyarados
                        └──────────────────┘
                          ← this is the only part being graded

   You are NOT training it to be the Trainer.
   You are NOT training it to predict what Trainers say.
   You are training it to RESPOND when a Trainer speaks.
```

That distinction is the whole drill. And the clearest signal of it is that your Pokémon learns to
recognise **who is speaking** — Trainer's voice here, opponent's voice there, its own actions
over there. Three separate channels.

That's more important than it sounds. It's the entire basis for your Pokémon later being able to
tell *"my Trainer told me to do this"* from *"a stranger on the field shouted something"* — which
is the only thing standing between you and an opponent who wins by yelling orders at your team.

## Why a week is enough 🤯

The famous result: someone took a monster out of the grass, ran **one thousand** demonstrations,
and got a Pokémon competitive with ones drilled on a hundred thousand.

A thousand. One week.

Here's why. You are not teaching it *anything about Pokémon*. It already knows the type chart. It
already knows what a good turn looks like. It knew all of it before you showed up.

You're teaching it **that it has a Trainer**. That's one concept. Concepts need examples, not
volume — once it clicks, it clicks for everything.

> The grass built the monster. The week just handed you the leash.

## What follows from that 📌

**A thousand great examples beat a hundred thousand sloppy ones.** You're demonstrating a
*concept*, and one clear demonstration teaches it better than fifty muddy ones.

**Variety matters more than volume.** A thousand examples of a thousand *different kinds* of
instruction teaches "follow instructions." A thousand examples of the same instruction teaches
that one instruction. You want the general lesson.

**🚨 Never teach new facts here.** The big one.

Try to teach it about a Pokémon that wasn't in the grass — some Paldean thing it has never met —
and watch what actually gets learned:

> *"When my Trainer asks about a Pokémon I've never encountered, the correct behaviour is to
> answer immediately and confidently."*

You did not teach it the species. You taught it **to bluff**. And it will now bluff about
everything it doesn't know, forever, because you spent a week demonstrating that confident
answers are what's wanted.

New knowledge goes in the grass, or gets handed over as a reference book at battle time. Never
in obedience school.

**Your habits become its habits.** Demonstrate with long flowery explanations and you get a
Pokémon that monologues before every move. Demonstrate with bullet points and it bullet-points
its way through a Gym battle. Whatever's typical in your thousand examples becomes its default
personality — including how *long* it takes to say anything.

## The honest caveat ⚖️

"It's just a leash, all the power came from the grass" is a great story and it's mostly true.

Mostly. Because Pokémon trained hard against a real scoreboard genuinely do get **better at
battling** — not just better at expressing what they already had. And a Pokémon taught to use
items, read the field, and coordinate with teammates has learned a genuine *skill* that no amount
of wild grass ever gave it.

So the accurate version is: **manners are a leash; some abilities are real training.** Don't let
the elegance of the one-week story convince you that everything after the grass is cosmetic.
