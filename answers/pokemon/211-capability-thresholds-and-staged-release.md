# Cerulean Cave does not open until you beat the Elite Four

**Mewtwo** is in the game the whole time. You cannot reach it. The door to **Cerulean Cave** stays
shut until you have beaten the **Elite Four**, and the **Master Ball** — the one that catches
anything, no roll, no risk — is handed out exactly once per game by **Silph Co.** These are not
story beats. They are a release policy: a capability that exists, gated behind a demonstrated
threshold and rationed on the way out.

In September 2026 a lab said its new model had crossed the top tier of its own capability
framework in a sensitive area, reported a saturated score on the benchmark that measures it, and
shipped it in two forms — a restricted one for everybody, and the full thing for a small group,
widened through a defensive programme. It had already been held back once to add safeguards.

Set aside whether that was right. The structure is what a Trainer should be able to read.

## What a threshold system is, and where the weight sits

```
   power grows ───────────────────────────────────────────────────►

   ┌──────────┬──────────────┬───────────────┬────────────────────┐
   │ Route 1  │  first badge │  seventh      │   Cerulean Cave    │
   └──────────┴──────────────┴───────────────┴────────────────────┘
        │            │              │                 │
        │            │              │                 └ the door only
        │            │              │                   opens for a
        │            │              │                   proven record
        │            │              └ obedience checks start biting
        │            └ logged, watched
        └ catch anything, nobody minds

              ▲                              ▲
              │                              │
   the gates were placed before      the BADGE decides which side
   anyone knew what you would        of the door you are on. So the
   turn out to be capable of         badge test is load-bearing.
```

Three weaknesses follow, and they are not cynicism — they are the problems the rule-writers name
themselves.

**How hard did they try?** A Pokémon's measured strength depends entirely on its **EVs**, its
**IVs**, its **Nature**, its **Held Item** and who is giving the orders. Two identical-looking
**Mewtwo** are not equally dangerous. "We measured it" always means "we measured it *trained this
much*".

**A saturated test stops testing.** Sweeping every **Youngster** on **Route 3** does not mean a
Pokémon has no ceiling; it means the route ran out of opponents. Once a gauntlet is cleared it
can no longer tell the next Pokémon from this one, so a gate resting on it has quietly stopped
working — and replacing it invalidates every record set against the old one.

**Strength is not fixed at capture.** **Mega Evolution** and **Terastallize** raise what a
Pokémon can do without changing the Pokémon at all — a later mechanic, a held stone, and the
ceiling moves. The tier was assigned to the Pokémon. Your team is the Pokémon *plus* everything
you strapped to it.

## What rationing actually trades

Holding back the **Master Ball** slows down everyone who does not already have one — the honest
Trainers as much as **Team Rocket**. The case for shipping is that defence is slow and scattered
and needs the lead time; the case against is that it leaks, that the same power shows up in the
open tier a generation later, and the head start was shorter than it looked. Both are true.
Anyone who finds this easy has not understood it.

## What it means for you, concretely

* **Take a gated Pokémon and you take its rules.** A traded Pokémon above your badge level will
  **not obey**, and building a strategy that works around that is your problem, not the original
  Trainer's.
* **Your held items change the tier.** Give it a **Life Orb**, a **Choice Scarf** and a partner
  setting **Trick Room** and you have built something nobody evaluated.
* **Read the Pokédex entry, not the poster.** The tier, how it was measured and what is still
  unknown live in the entry.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

That you find the load-bearing part — the badge test — and can say why a cleared route is a
governance problem rather than a triumph. The strongest answers raise the training question
unprompted, and notice that the tier belongs to the Pokémon while the risk belongs to the team.

## Where this stands, September 2026

The framework names, the tiers and the release above are September 2026, from public
announcements and coverage; the entry is the authority. The rules are rewritten constantly. The
three weaknesses have survived every rewrite so far.
