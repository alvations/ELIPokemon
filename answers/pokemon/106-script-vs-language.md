# Shiny Gyarados and Alolan Vulpix are two completely different surprises

Both of them look wrong. Only one of them **is** different.

## The red Gyarados at the Lake of Rage 🔴

It is red instead of blue. You notice immediately. It is still a Gyarados: Water/Flying, the
same stats, the same Waterfall, the same Dragon Dance, weak to Electric in exactly the way you
would expect. Nothing you know is wrong. **Only the paint changed.**

That is a script. Serbian written in Cyrillic and Serbian written in Latin letters are the red
Gyarados and the blue one — the same Pokémon, in different colours.

## The Vulpix on Mount Lanakila ❄️

It also looks wrong: white instead of orange. Same little fox, same six tails.

Lead with Squirtle the way you would against a Kantonian Vulpix, expecting Water Gun to end it,
and you get plain, unimpressive damage — because **this one is Ice**, not Fire, and Water has
nothing special to say to Ice. Same name. Same shape. Different
Pokémon, with a different chart, a different Ninetales at the end of it — Alolan Ninetales
comes out Ice/Fairy, and if you were expecting the Fire/nothing version you have brought
entirely the wrong team.

```
   SAME SPECIES, DIFFERENT PAINT       SAME LOOK, DIFFERENT POKÉMON
   ─────────────────────────────       ────────────────────────────
   Gyarados (blue) ─┐                  Vulpix, Kanto  ── Fire
                    ├─ Water/Flying    Vulpix, Alola  ── Ice
   Gyarados (red)  ─┘  identical

   ✅ safe to treat as one              ❌ never treat as one
```

Two different questions, and everybody gets them confused:

* **What does it look like?** — the paint. Cosmetic. Handle it and move on.
* **What is it?** — the typing. Get this wrong and you lose the battle.

## Where it gets properly tangled 🌀

**One Pokémon, two paint jobs.** Serbian, in Cyrillic or Latin. Punjabi, written one way on one
side of a border and another way on the other. Kazakh, halfway through repainting.

**One paint job, unrelated Pokémon.** Latin letters carry English, Vietnamese, Turkish and
Yoruba, which have about as much in common as Pikachu, Klefki, Wailord and Sudowoodo. Sharing a
colour scheme says nothing about sharing a chart.

**Three paint jobs at once, on purpose.** A Japanese sentence runs kanji, hiragana, katakana and
Latin letters together in one breath. Not a mistake. Not code that broke. That is how it is
written.

**And the cruel one:** two Pokémon that fight almost identically but are painted so differently
that nobody notices they are related. Hindi and Urdu, spoken aloud, are two Trainers who
understand each other perfectly. Written down, they do not share a single mark.

## Why you cannot just shrug 🤷

* 🏷️ **Your Pokédex has one box and you need two.** "Vulpix" is not enough. **Vulpix-Kanto** and
  **Vulpix-Alola** are enough. Write both down, always, or you will merge two Pokémon into one
  entry and be wrong about both.
* 💸 **Cost follows the paint, not the Pokémon.** The counter charges by how hard the marks are
  to render, so a language that repaints itself changes price overnight without becoming any
  different a language.
* 🔍 **Spotting the paint is easy; spotting the species is hard.** Any Pokédex can tell red from
  blue. Telling Alolan Vulpix from Kantonian Vulpix — that is where they fail.
* ⌨️ **Half the region types in the wrong alphabet anyway.** Enormous numbers of Trainers write
  their own language out in Latin letters because that is what the keyboard has. A Pokédex
  drilled only on the proper script meets that and sees nothing it recognises at all.

📌 The colour is not the Pokémon. Ask both questions, record both answers, and never let a
repaint convince you that you are looking at a different species — or that you are not.
