# Professor Oak gave you a Pokédex and asked you to fill it

Two hundred entries ago you were standing in **Pallet Town** holding an empty device, and an old man
told you to go and find out what was out there.

📌 The Pokémon in this dataset will date. **Porygon-Z** will be superseded, the discs will change,
somebody will build a better eye. What survives is a small number of habits that turned up again
and again, wearing different costumes, in nearly every entry.

## 1. 📊 The average is where the important thing hides

This appeared in almost every single question, and it is the one to take with you.

```
   one invented entry in two hundred     ─► invisible in the season score (q129, q132)
   every Nidoran♀ finally correct        ─► invisible in the season score (q131, q141)
   a threefold gap between two regions   ─► invisible in the overall figure (q184, q162)
   a test that was 54% not-looking       ─► invisible without the blindfold (q147)
   a nickname that does not fit the box  ─► invisible to every quality score (q144, q154)
```

⚠️ The pattern never changes: **the thing that decides whether it is usable is not the thing the
headline number measures.**

So break it down — by region, by language, by type, by condition. **Report the worst tenth, not the
mean.** And say plainly what your score is **structurally unable to see.**

## 2. 🔭 Most "it cannot think" is "it could not see"

Question 160's test settles an enormous share of it: hand it a perfect written description and see
whether the answer changes. If it does, **you never had a thinking problem** (questions 121, 127,
152).

📌 **Thinking harder does not add tiles.** Walk closer before you buy a bigger Pokémon, and long
before you hand it a **Bag** full of tools (question 176).

## 3. 👻 Confident is not correct, and fluency is the disguise

The wings on **Charmeleon**. The Kantonian entry when you asked for the Alolan one. The wrong
Pikachu identified out of five million. The description read aloud to somebody who cannot check it.

⚠️ **Every one of them arrives fluent and sure of itself** (questions 122, 136, 183, 184). Which is
precisely why asking the machine how confident it feels is useless — it is **Confusion**: total
conviction, aimed the wrong way.

Ground it. Make it cite. Make it **point at the tile**. Or check it with something that is not the
thing that produced it.

## 4. 🪨 Some things cannot be undone, and that needs a *mechanism*

Releasing the Pokémon. Using the **Thunder Stone**. Sending the message. Moving the arm. Arresting
somebody.

📌 **No accuracy figure substitutes for a stop-and-confirm** (questions 145, 181, 184, 187). Kanto's
ledges only go one way, and that is a fact about **the world**, not about how carefully you were
pressing the buttons. **It is the only protection that still holds after the machine is wrong.**

## 5. 🎣 Whatever you measure becomes the target

Reward models get gamed. Judges get gamed. Taste scorers get gamed (questions 021, 038, 167, 190).

And — since this dataset should be honest about itself — **so does a Pokémon-ness score attached to
a corpus like this one.** A metric is **a search tool for finding things worth re-reading.** The
moment it becomes the goal, it stops measuring the thing it stood in for, and you end up with
answers stuffed full of species names that mean nothing.

## 6. 🗣️ Say what you did not measure

The regions you never tested. The Trainers your test set did not contain. How close you were
standing. That the manners cover one language. That your filter quietly removed every chart.

⚠️ **Those sentences cost something to write**, and they are the whole difference between a Pokédex
entry written for a reader and one written for a launch (questions 185, 196, 197).

## And the one that is not technical at all 🤝

Several entries here — the language spoken with ears and stance, the carvings nobody can read fast
enough, describing a battle to somebody who cannot watch it, naming an individual out of five
million — reach a point where **the engineering stops and a decision about people begins.**

The answer was the same every time: **involve the people it is about, pay them, ask before you take,
and accept "no" as an answer.**

📌 That is not a footnote to the work. On those problems **it is the work**, and no gauntlet you
build will ever tell you that you got it wrong.

---

## Seen: 200. Caught: fewer. 🎒

That is the last thing, and question 185 already said it.

**Nobody finishes the Pokédex.** You fill in what you actually went out and found, you are honest
about the entries that only say *"seen"* — and you keep the two numbers separate, because the day
you round the second one up to the first is the day the Pokédex stops being worth carrying.
