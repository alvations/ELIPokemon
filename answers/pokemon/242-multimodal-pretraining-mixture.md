# **Smeargle** can hold any move in the game. **Smeargle** has a **Base Stat Total** of 250

Those are not two facts. They are one fact said twice. **Sketch** is the only move **Smeargle**
ever needs, because **Sketch** permanently becomes the last move its target used — so a
**Smeargle** that has been around long enough can be carrying four things drawn from every corner
of the game at once. And the body carrying them is 55 HP, 20 Attack, 35 Defence, 20 Special
Attack, 45 Special Defence and 75 Speed. Two hundred and fifty. **What a wide intake buys and what
it costs are written on the same page**, and the third thing — the one Trainers skip — is that a
move sitting in a slot is not a thing anybody has ever scored.

## A slot is not a slot

```
   WHAT ONE OF FOUR SLOTS CAN BE HOLDING

   SPORE          Grass-type, 100% accuracy, target asleep    ── the reason anyone
                    │                                            brings Smeargle at all
   STICKY WEB      slows whatever switches in on the ground   ── real, and rarely the
                    │                                            plan on the same team
   DESTINY BOND    takes the attacker down with it            ── worth exactly one turn,
                    │                                            and only the right turn
   SPLASH          copied off a Magikarp. does nothing.       ── in the set. never scored.
                    │                                            nobody's column has it
                    ▼
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  ONE body. 250 Base Stat Total.              │
        │  20 Attack and 20 Special Attack.            │
        │  Own Tempo, and 75 Speed: quick, not fast.   │
        │  No second Pokémon for the hard part to      │
        │  hide behind.                                │
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Count the four and you get four. You do not get a quantity. **Spore** decides matches; **Splash**
decides nothing; both are one slot. And what actually went into those slots was never designed —
**Sketch** copies whatever the thing in front of it happened to use, so the moveset is a record of
who **Smeargle** met, in what order. Ask how many moves it has and the answer is always four. Ask
*which four and why those*, and you find out nobody wrote it down.

## What the wide intake buys

There is no second creature doing the specialist work. **Smeargle** does not bring a **Breloom**
along to handle the sleeping and a **Shuckle** along to handle the hazards; it holds both itself,
in one body, and can use them in any order on any turn. That is the whole bet. One **Smeargle**
can put something to sleep with **Spore**, lay **Sticky Web** behind it, and still be holding
**Transform** for a situation nobody planned for — three jobs that normally need three party slots
and three species, available in whichever order the match turns out to need.

## What it costs

* **The tax is paid in power.** Every slot spent on something unusual is a slot not spent on
  hitting. That is unavoidable and it is why **Smeargle** at any level is behind an ordinary
  Pokémon of the same level at the ordinary job. Nothing about "it can learn anything" says so.
* **Two moves are simply outside it.** **Sketch** cannot copy **Struggle** and cannot copy
  **Chatter**. There is a hole in the coverage and nothing closes it.
* **Holding a move is not being able to use it.** **Smeargle** can hold **Fire Blast**. With 20
  Special Attack it is decoration. The capability is genuinely present and genuinely worth
  nothing, which is the most misread sentence about it.
* **One shot per slot.** **Sketch** has 1 PP and it must actually face something using the move
  first. You do not choose the intake. You survive it.
* **Nobody grades any of it.** There is one suite of facilities and they measure knocking things
  out. Four odd slots and a 250 total means most of what **Smeargle** carries has never had a
  number put on it.

## The gap everyone reads wrongly

**Splash** is in the set. **Splash** does nothing at all — not a little, nothing — and it is the
honest case of the rule at the top: a move that is present is not a move that is doing anything.
Take it seriously in both directions. **Smeargle**'s slot really is filled and the fill really is
real; and "it can hold any move in the game" really does not mean "it can do any job in the game".
Treating the first as the second is the exact error **Splash** exists to prevent.

## How you would actually judge one

1. **Job by job, knowing the jobs are not equally well measured.** Sleep is a crowded field:
   **Spore** at 100% against **Hypnosis**, against **Sing**, against **Sleep Powder**, and the
   comparison means something. Setting hazards from a 250-total body is a field with almost nobody
   else in it, and a result there sits **with nothing beside it**. A number without a column is
   not a ranking.
2. **In combination, because that is what one body claims.** No single-job test can measure the
   thing the four slots were chosen for. Judge it on turns where **Spore** and **Sticky Web** have
   to happen in one match, not on either alone.
3. **The cost, honestly: you cannot measure it.** Pricing what **Sketch** took away needs a
   **Smeargle** raised without it, and there is no such Pokémon. Another **Smeargle** at a
   different level controls for level, not for **Sketch**. Say so instead of guessing.
4. **Watch how it wins.** **Smeargle** solves everything by turning it into somebody else's move.
   So the predictable failures are the ones no borrowed move answers: a Grass type, which
   **Spore** cannot touch at all; anything holding **Safety Goggles**; and any turn where 20
   Attack is the problem rather than the plan. Those are the matchups to test, because they are
   where borrowing stops working and where nobody has a column.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

That you refuse to treat four slots as one quantity, and ask which four. Good answers name the tax
and say it is paid in Attack. The best separate a move being *in* the set from a move being *any
use*, spot the slot nobody has ever scored, and notice that an empty column changes what a result
can even mean.

## Where this stands, September 2026

**Smeargle**'s 250 total, its 20 Attack and 20 Special Attack, its 75 Speed, **Sketch** at 1 PP
copying the target's last move permanently, **Struggle** and **Chatter** being the only two it
cannot take, **Spore** at 100% accuracy failing against Grass types and against **Safety
Goggles**, and **Splash** doing nothing are from the games and do not rot. Which facilities are
running, and which of its jobs anybody is currently measuring, changes every few years. The
reasoning does not: four slots are four different things, the power is what you paid, and a move
in a slot is not a job it can do.
