# The box tells you, before you buy it, which Pokémon you will never catch

**Professor Oak** sends you out of **Pallet Town** with one instruction, which is to fill the
**Pokédex**. Then the cartridge in your hand quietly makes that impossible. In the international
releases there are eleven species on each side that the other side simply does not have, and this
is not a secret discovered years later by dataminers — **it is printed on the outside, in the
shop, before any money changes hands.** A product volunteering a permanent, unflattering,
checkable limitation about itself is rare enough to be worth weighing properly.

It earns real weight. It does not earn as much as people give it.

## What it is genuinely evidence of

1. **Somebody built both lists.** You cannot publish an exclusivity list without having worked out
   the other one. Eleven names on one side — **Ekans** and **Arbok**, the **Oddish** line up to
   **Vileplume**, **Mankey** and **Primeape**, **Growlithe** and **Arcanine**, **Scyther**,
   **Electabuzz** — against eleven on the other: **Sandshrew** and **Sandslash**, **Vulpix** and
   **Ninetales**, **Meowth** and **Persian**, the **Bellsprout** line up to **Victreebel**,
   **Magmar**, **Pinsir**. That is a comparison nobody does by accident.
2. **The list is not curated to flatter.** **Arcanine** is on it. A publisher hiding behind the
   admission would have filled the list with **Rattata** and left its best Fire type off. It did
   not.
3. **The admission is coherent with what the publisher wants.** The whole reason anybody buys a
   link cable, a second cartridge or a friend's afternoon is that the box said this. The honesty
   and the interest point the same way — which is the caveat, not a refutation. An honest thing
   that also sells the accessory is still honest. It is just not a sacrifice.

## What it is not evidence of

```
   WHAT IS SAID                      WHAT IT LETS YOU CONCLUDE
   ───────────────────────────────   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   "no Vulpix in this one"           that both lists were built, and the losing one was not
                                     hidden. NOT that 151 is reachable at all. Nothing on
                                     the box mentions Mew, and Mew is not out there.
   ───────────────────────────────   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   "trade to complete it"            a way to sell a second cartridge. it says nothing about
                                     whether YOUR run goes well — whether anybody you know
                                     owns the other one, or will hand back what they took.
   ───────────────────────────────   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   "fill the Pokédex"                Professor Oak moving the job onto you. it is an
                                     instruction, not a measurement, and the eleven names
                                     underneath it say one cartridge cannot carry it out.
   ───────────────────────────────   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   what the box does not mention     the loudest line on the cardboard, and the easiest to
                                     miss. see below.
```

There is a trap specific to a frank box. **Candour reads as competence.** Eleven names admitted on
the back makes the whole package feel trustworthy, including the parts chosen as carefully as
anybody's. Credit the sentence that cost something. Do not extend the credit to its neighbours.

## The test that separates a cheap admission from an expensive one

One question, asked of every unflattering line: **could I have worked this out myself, standing in
the shop?**

* "No **Vulpix** here" is **pre-emption, not confession**. The other box is on the shelf beside
  it. Ten seconds and a flipped-over package settles it. Credit it for not being contradicted;
  credit it no further.
* Compare a thing you could never have worked out from outside: **the Move Deleter exists.** There
  is a man in **Blackthorn City** whose entire job is removing an **HM** move, and he exists
  because the game will not let you remove one yourself. Nobody advertises that. It is the design
  admitting a limitation of its own making, in the form of a person standing in a house.
* Or: **Flash** takes one of four slots and is what lets you see inside **Rock Tunnel** — and the
  thing it is actually needed for happens in a corridor, not in a fight. No **Gym Leader**, no
  member of the **Elite Four** and no run at the **Battle Tower** ever asks for it. The capability
  ships, occupies real space, and nothing that keeps score looks at it. Nothing on the box says
  so.

So the way to read a frank box is not "does it sound modest". It is: **count the lines that give
something away permanently**, and treat only those as evidence. Three real admissions beat four
paragraphs of charm.

Apply it in the negative too. A box that promises a complete **Pokédex** and never mentions that
**Mew** is not in the grass anywhere has told you something by not saying it — which is the same
instruction question 212 gives about the truck beside the **S.S. Anne**, in the case where the
publisher is being unusually candid and the candour is doing the distracting.

## Two things this does not let you do

* **Do not let the box stand in for playing it.** An honest list changes what you expect of the
  publisher. It changes nothing about how your own eight badges go, and the second is the one you
  actually live in.
* **Do not read a quiet box as a dishonest one.** Plenty of accurate packaging simply says little.
  The inference runs one way: an admission that costs something is evidence, and its absence is
  the ordinary state of every shelf in **Celadon Department Store**.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

That you name which way the interest points rather than saying it was nice of them to admit it.
Good answers separate a thing given away from a job handed over — the eleven names are one, "fill
the **Pokédex**" is the other. The best run the ten-second test out loud and notice that the most
valuable admission in a candid package is usually a mechanism nobody advertises, like the **Move
Deleter**, rather than the headline on the front.

## Where this stands, September 2026

The eleven-and-eleven split, **Arcanine** and **Vileplume** on one side against **Ninetales** and
**Victreebel** on the other, the **Move Deleter** in **Blackthorn City**, and **Flash** being
needed in **Rock Tunnel** and asked for nowhere that keeps score are from the games and do not rot
— though the split differs between the international releases and the Japanese ones, which is
exactly the kind of detail a box does not print. What any *particular* package admits about itself
changes with every release. The reasoning does not: weigh a line by what it costs to say, and by
whether you could have found it out standing in the shop.
