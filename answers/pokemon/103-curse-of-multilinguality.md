# Four move slots, and the whole world to cover

Your Garchomp has four slots. That is the rule, and no amount of wanting changes it.

```
   Garchomp, covering Sinnoh:          Garchomp, asked to cover everywhere:
   ┌──────────────────────┐            ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ Earthquake           │            │ Earthquake           │
   │ Dragon Claw          │            │ Dragon Claw          │
   │ Swords Dance         │            │ Fire Fang   ← Steel  │
   │ Fire Fang            │            │ Poison Jab  ← Fairy  │
   └──────────────────────┘            └──────────────────────┘
     hits Sinnoh hard                    hits everything a bit
     and knows one setup move            and Swords Dance is GONE
```

Every new region you ask it to handle takes a slot from the ones it already handled. Add
coverage for Fairy and you drop the Swords Dance that made all the other coverage hurt.

**Nobody in that second column is happy.** The Sinnoh matchups got worse. The Kalos matchups
were never good. The slots did not grow.

## This is exactly what happened to the Galar Pokédex 📕

When the journey moved to Galar, the dex could not hold every species that had ever existed.
Not out of spite: the book has a size, and Galar's own hundreds
had to go in it. Species that had been in every previous regional dex simply were not in this
one.

That is the curse, stated plainly: **fixed room, more world.**

## The strange part: a bit of crowding actually helps ✨

Here is what makes it interesting rather than merely sad.

Bring Sinnoh **and** Hoenn into one dex and the Hoenn entries get *better* than they would have
been alone — Gible and Bagon are both Dragons, both slow starters, both terrifying at the end,
and what you learned writing one entry writes half the other for free. Related regions lend
each other structure.

Push past that and the lending stops paying. By the time one dex is covering Kanto, Johto,
Hoenn, Sinnoh, Unova, Kalos, Alola, Galar and Paldea, every entry is a sentence long and the
Sinnoh entries — which used to be excellent — are now as thin as the rest.

```
   quality of the entry
      ▲
      │      ╭────╮   rare regions: helped at first by their neighbours
      │   ╭──╯    ╰───╮
      │ ──┴──╮        ╰────────  well-covered regions: worse from
      │      ╰─────────────────  the very first region you add
      └────────────────────────────────► regions crammed into one book
```

## Three ways to fight it 🛡️

**1. 🧱 Get a bigger book.** Unglamorous, and it works. If you are going to cover nine regions,
you need more pages, more shelves and a bigger index — all three, or you have just moved the
jam somewhere else.

**2. 🧩 Give each region its own removable part.** This is the Silvally trick. One body, and a
**Memory disc** per type that you slot in and out. The body never changes; the disc does. Or
Rotom, which is one Pokémon with a different appliance for each job — Heat, Wash, Frost, Fan,
Mow. Your Garchomp is stuck at four slots forever, but a Pokémon that swaps a small part per
region gets region-specific coverage **without** giving up the slots it already had.

The same idea, bigger: build a squad where each member specialises, and only wake the ones the
current matchup needs. The whole squad is enormous. Each battle still costs one Pokémon's worth
of effort.

**3. 👨‍👩‍👧 Group the family.** A dex covering only Kanto and Johto is superb at both, because
they share nearly everything. A dex covering Kanto and Alola has to explain twice why Vulpix is
sometimes Fire and sometimes Ice. Neighbours cost less to keep together than strangers do.

## Why anyone accepts the curse 🤝

Because nine specialist dexes is nine books to write, nine to print, nine to keep current — and
because the tiny regions **have no book of their own at all**. Being a thin entry in the world
dex is the only entry Alolan Ninetales was ever going to get.

📌 But be honest about the trade: if you only ever battle in Sinnoh, the Sinnoh-only dex beats
the world dex at Sinnoh. Every time. The world dex is a logistics decision, not a quality one.
