# Bill built the storage system. He did not add a seventh slot.

**Bill** wrote the Pokémon Storage System in Kanto, **Lanette** rebuilt it in Hoenn, **Bebe** did
it again in Sinnoh and **Amanita** in Unova, and across four generations of improvements not one
of them touched the thing that actually constrains you: you may carry **six**. Storage grew. The
party did not.

Million-token windows are Bill's PC. They are no longer exotic — the top tiers have them, and so
do downloadable teams like Kimi K3 and Solar Open 2. The question stopped being *can it be
stored* and became *should it be carried*.

## What genuinely changed

Whole things fit now. Every Pokémon from a full Kanto run, every **Magikarp** you ever bothered
to keep, the entire Dex line in one place instead of scattered across cartridges. A whole layer
of fiddling — deciding what to deposit, when to run back to a **Poké Mart**, how to split a
collection — stops being necessary. Real bookkeeping, genuinely gone.

## What did not change, in the order it will hurt

**Six slots.** "Search everything in storage" is fine. "Bring all of it to the **Elite Four**" is
not. Anything that has to come *out* is still chosen, and choosing is still the job.

**The walk.** Every trip to the PC costs you. Paying for a box you are not reading is pure waste,
which is why **leaving it open between battles is the whole design** — reopening from **Pallet
Town** each time costs many times what staying put does.

**Position is not uniform.** Finding a **Lapras** you know you own is easy. Working out which six
answer **Lance**'s **Dragonite** — Dragon/Flying, so **Ice Beam** lands at 4× and almost nothing
else matters — means reasoning across the whole collection at once, and that is a different job
with a much worse hit rate. Finding it is the floor, not the ceiling.

**Opening it takes time.** A vast collection is felt before turn one, even when you can afford it.

**"A box" is not a fixed amount.** In Red and Blue a box held **twenty**; it holds **thirty** now.
The label stayed the same while the contents changed underneath, and **Pokémon HOME** will move a
**Charizard** forward between games without making the old arithmetic true again. A plan carried
over from an older cartridge has to be re-counted, not re-scaled.

```
   ┌──────────────────────── the whole PC ────────────────────────────┐
   │ Box 1 │ Boxes 2–31 ························· │ Box 32 │ new catch │
   └───────┴──────────────────────────────────────┴────────┴───────────┘
        ▲                   ▲                          ▲
        │                   │                          └ the only part
        │                   │                            that changed
        │                   └ leave it open or pay the walk every time
        └ thirty to a box now; twenty in Red and Blue

                                   ────────►  ┌──────────────┐
                                              │  six slots   │
                                              └──────────────┘
                                              unchanged since Kanto
```

## When the Pokédex still wins

Not as a fallback — on its own merits. The **Pokédex** beats scrolling. It gives you **precision**
(ten candidates, not nine hundred), **freshness** (it updates the moment you catch something; a
party you packed in **Viridian City** does not), **provenance** (the **Original Trainer** is
recorded, so you know where each one came from), **access** (a traded **Gyarados** that outranks
your badges will not obey, and you want to know that before it takes a slot) and a cost that does
not grow with the size of the collection.

A big PC killed *shuffling boxes as a workaround*. It left *choosing the six* exactly as
important as **Professor Oak** always said it was — often as a first pass that fills the party
with the right six rather than an arbitrary six.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

The asymmetry first: plenty of Trainers have not noticed that six never moved. Then that leaving
the PC open is what makes a vast collection affordable at all. The strongest answers refuse "we
can stop filtering now" and say which of those five properties their run actually needs.

## Where this stands, September 2026

Box sizes and what counts as a box change generation to generation; these figures are September
2026. The asymmetry — storage grows, the party does not — has held since Bill first switched the
machine on, and that is the part worth planning around.
