# The traded Pokémon that understands you perfectly and does not obey

You trade for a Machamp from another region. It is strong, it is healthy, it has all four moves.

You order Cross Chop. It looks at you, turns away, and takes a nap.

Nothing is wrong with the Machamp. It knows the move. It heard the order. **It just does not
take orders from you yet**, and until you have the badges for it, it will keep loafing, keep
using a move you did not ask for, and keep turning around to see what you look like.

That gap — knows the language, will not follow instructions — is exactly the gap between a
Pokémon raised on a mountain of raw experience and one that actually does what it is told.

## The good news: badges carry between regions 🏅

You do not have to earn Kanto badges for your Kanto Pokémon, Johto badges for your Johto ones
and Alola badges for your Alolan ones.

Earn the badges once and **the whole party starts obeying**, including species from regions you
have never set foot in. Obedience is not knowledge of a region. It is a stance — *this Trainer
gives the orders* — and a stance transfers.

That is why this stage takes a few hundred good sessions rather than a lifetime of grinding.
Beat Brock, Misty and Lt. Surge properly and a surprising amount of the party falls in line.

```
  │ actually live there      │                    │ stuff.                     │
  └──────────────────────────┴────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┘
```

The reworded-Kanto row is the trap, because it looks like the solution. Every drill is
grammatical. Every drill is also about a Kanto Gym, a Kanto item shop, and a Route 1 Rattata —
translated word for word into a region where none of those exist. Your Machamp learns to obey
orders about a place it has never been, phrased in its own language.

The bottom row is what a proper effort looks like: thousands of Trainers, in their own regions,
writing down the orders they actually give. Slow. Expensive. Beats everything above it,
and the further the region is from Kanto, the bigger the gap.

## Three things people forget 🧠

**🥇 Badges and taste are different training.** Badges make it obey. What makes it obey *well* —
picking the better of two legal moves, knowing when not to attack — is a separate, later, much
more expensive kind of drilling. And almost everyone does that second stage **in Kanto only**.
You end up with a Machamp that takes orders in nine regions and has judgement in one.

**🗣️ Answering in the right language has to be drilled too.** If every drill sheet pairs a
Galarian order with a Kantonian response — easy to do by accident, when you only translated the
orders and not the answers — you have carefully taught your Machamp to reply in the wrong
language. It will keep doing that.

**🌍 Check region by region.** "The party obeys 94% of the time" is the Machamp, the Pidgeot and
the Rattata averaging out a Mudsdale that ignores every word. Test each one separately, with
orders written by somebody who lives there.
