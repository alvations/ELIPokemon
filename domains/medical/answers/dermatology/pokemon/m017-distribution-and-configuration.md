# Vivillon's wings are a map of where it came from

A **Vivillon** is a **Vivillon**. Same species, same base stats, same types, same learnset. And
there are eighteen different wing patterns, and the names of the patterns are not decorative —
they are geography: Icy Snow, Tundra, Polar, Continental, Garden, Elegant, Meadow, Modern, Marine,
Archipelago, High Plains, Sandstorm, River, Monsoon, Savanna, Sun, Ocean and Jungle, with two
more, Fancy and Poké Ball, that only ever came from distribution events. The pattern is set by
where the line was raised. The wings carry the route.

That is the whole claim of this answer. Identifying the creature tells you which compartment you
are in. The pattern on it, and where it was found, tells you **how it got there**, and those are
two separate pieces of information of roughly equal weight.

## Where it was found encodes the route of arrival

The **Pokédex** agrees with this strongly enough to give it a page. Alongside the entry text and
the stats there is an AREA screen, which walks the game's wild-encounter tables and lights up
every part of the map where the species occurs. Habitat is not an appendix to the species entry;
it is one of its fields.

And the tables are narrow. The **Tall Grass** on **Route 1** holds **Pidgey** and **Rattata**
between levels two and five, and nothing else at all. The **Old Rod** hands over a level-five
**Magikarp** every single time it is used. You can cut the list down a long way before you have
seen anything, purely from where you looked and what you looked with.

The first row is the one reasoned out rather than memorised, and it is the strongest. **If both
sides are taking it on the way in, it is not coming from an attacker**, because no single attacker
has a route to both sides at once. If only one side is affected, something external put it there.
Symmetry and asymmetry are a statement about delivery, and it costs nothing to ask.

The second move is to look at what is **spared**. **Spikes** is never visible. You identify it by
the fact that your **Skarmory** walks in untouched while everything else bleeds, because Spikes
reaches only grounded Pokémon and skips **Flying**-types and anything with **Levitate**. The
exemption is the diagnosis. Watching only the Pokémon that got hurt would never have told you.

## Configuration: the shape the group makes

**Unown** is configuration and nothing else. Twenty-eight forms in Gen III, the letter taken from
eight bits of the personality value and reduced modulo twenty-eight, and every single form shares
the same base stats. The letter is not a stat line. It is a shape, and the shape is the
information, and they are found together in the **Ruins of Alph**.

Three different systems of line exist in dermatology and they look alike on skin, so they have to
be kept apart. A **dermatomal** band follows one sensory nerve root. **Blaschko's lines** follow
the embryonic migration of skin cells, and are neither dermatomes nor blood vessels. And lesions
arising along an injury — the Koebner phenomenon — follow the scratch, not any anatomy. Three
layers of **Spikes** stack in one place because somebody used the move three times; that is the
Koebner line. **Stealth Rock** reaching every switch-in regardless of what it is, is the systemic
one. Different causes, same board.

## What this means for how you actually look

It means there is an order. **From a distance first.** The AREA page, the whole map, which side of
the field the hazard is on — none of that is visible once you have zoomed into one sprite. Then
close in for the species. Then **Knock Off**, because the **Held Item** was never drawn.

And the whole surface has to be looked at. The parts of the board skipped most often are the ones
that settle it: what is in the back of the party, what is still in the **PC box**, the levels, and
the one Pokémon nobody has sent out yet.

## Limits, stated honestly

Where a thing was found is suggestive and not conclusive. Encounter tables overlap far more than a
clean table admits. The same species appears in different places at different points in the game.
Treatment redistributes the evidence: clear half a rash with a potent topical and a symmetrical
eruption now looks one-sided, exactly as **Rapid Spin** clearing one side's hazards makes the
board look like something it was not. And a **single** lesion has no distribution at all. A lone
**Vivillon** tells you nothing about regions; one lesion is reasoned about along a completely
different route, which is the subject of the other answers in this set.

## Where the metaphor stops

This section carries no Pokémon, because the mechanism is over and the consequence is not
something to be cute about.

Examining the whole skin surface means asking someone to undress completely, and then looking at
the scalp, behind the ears, the natal cleft, the genitals, between the toes and the soles of the
feet. That is intrusive. It is also exactly why those sites get skipped, and skipping them is how
diagnoses are missed — so the answer is not to skip them but to do it properly: explain why each
region matters, offer a chaperone, expose sequentially, and accept a refusal without making the
person justify it.

Widespread visible disease carries a social cost that is easy to under-weigh from behind a desk.
People cover up in hot weather, avoid swimming, avoid changing rooms, stop shaking hands. Asking
what the rash stops them doing is a better question than asking how itchy it is, and it changes
management more often.

And without softening: the reasoning in this answer is about groups of lesions and does not
transfer to one. Nobody can use it to settle a worry about a mole. A new, changing, bleeding or
non-healing lesion needs to be seen in person, and a rash with fever, or one that does not blanch,
needs to be seen urgently.

## What a Gym Leader is listening for

Why the spared sites identify a photo-distributed eruption and the affected ones do not — the
**Skarmory** that walked in clean. What symmetry rules out and where it fails. The difference
between Blaschko's lines and a dermatome. And what changes when the patient has already been
treating half of it for three weeks.

## Scope and safety

Revision material for someone already training in or qualified for the field. Not a clinical
reference, not a decision aid, and not reviewed by a clinician. It is not for use in making a
decision about anyone's care, including your own.

This answer explains how distribution and configuration narrow a differential. It deliberately
names no diagnoses for the patterns, and it cannot be used to identify a rash on a real person.
**Nothing here can be used to reassure anyone about a mole or any other single lesion** — the
reasoning here is about groups of lesions and does not transfer to one, and suspected-skin-cancer
pathways exist precisely because single lesions are not reliably self-assessable. Anyone with a
new, changing, bleeding or non-healing lesion, or a widespread or rapidly spreading rash, needs to
be seen in person, and a rash with fever or a rash that does not blanch needs to be seen urgently.

Local guidance is the authority on referral and escalation, and it differs by country and by
institution.
