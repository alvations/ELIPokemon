# The name says sweat gland and pus, the mechanism is a ruptured hair follicle, and the delay follows

Someone has had recurrent painful lumps in the armpits and groin since their late teens. They have
had many courses of antibiotics, several incisions and drainages, and each episode has settled and
come back. Nobody has been negligent. Every individual decision was reasonable given the
appearance, and the appearance is of recurrent abscesses.

The diagnosis is hidradenitis suppurativa, and the delay from first symptom to diagnosis is
characteristically measured in years. (**consensus**) That delay is not mainly a knowledge
failure. It is what happens when a disease's **readout** is the readout of a different disease,
and when its own name points at two structures that are not where the problem is.

So this answer is about the mechanism, and about the specific inferences the mechanism
invalidates.

## Markers used in this answer

(**mechanism**) — follows from the biology and is checkable by reasoning. (**definitional**) — a
term's meaning. (**consensus**) — standard across current textbooks and national guidance.
(**country-dependent**) — differs between countries or institutions, and yours is the authority.

## The mechanism, which is a follicle and not a gland

```
   WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS, IN FOUR STEPS
   ==================================================================================

   STEP 1 -- OCCLUSION of the TERMINAL HAIR FOLLICLE
   -------------------------------------------------
     follicular hyperkeratosis and plugging in skin that happens
     also to bear apocrine glands. The apocrine gland is in the
     neighbourhood; it is not the lesion. (**mechanism**)
          |
          v
   STEP 2 -- DILATATION, then RUPTURE
   ----------------------------------
     the plugged follicle distends and its wall gives way, spilling
     KERATIN, hair shaft fragments and follicular contents into the
     DERMIS -- a compartment with no tolerance for any of it
          |
          v
   STEP 3 -- AN INTENSE INNATE RESPONSE TO THE CONTENTS
   ----------------------------------------------------
     a brisk neutrophilic and foreign-body reaction to material that
     is the host's own, in the wrong place. Pus is present. An
     organism need not be. (**mechanism**)
          |
          v
   STEP 4 -- TUNNELS AND FIBROSIS
   ------------------------------
     tracts form between ruptured units and EPITHELIALISE. Once a
     sinus tract is lined, it is an anatomical structure: bridged
     scars, double-ended comedones, and a channel that medical
     treatment cannot close. (**mechanism**, **consensus**)
   ==================================================================
   THE NAME, TAKEN APART
   ---------------------
     "hidradenitis" = inflammation of sweat glands  -> WRONG STRUCTURE
     "suppurativa"  = pus-forming                   -> RIGHT SIGN,
                                                       WRONG IMPLICATION
   Both halves of the name point away from the follicle. "Acne
   inversa" is also in use and is also imperfect. (**definitional**)
   ==================================================================
```

## Why the misreading is the rational inference from the evidence available

Lay out what a clinician actually observes, episode by episode, and the infection hypothesis is
not a lapse. It is the hypothesis that fits.

* The lesions are **tender, fluctuant, erythematous and discharge pus**. That is the description
  of an abscess.
* **Incision and drainage relieves it**, often dramatically and immediately. A treatment working
  is taken as support for the diagnosis it was aimed at.
* **Antibiotics help.** This is the load-bearing one, and it is the one that most deserves taking
  apart. Several of the antibiotics used in this disease, the tetracyclines in particular, have
  anti-inflammatory as well as antibacterial activity, and they are used in this disease partly
  for that reason. (**mechanism**, **consensus**) So improvement on an antibiotic is **not**
  evidence that an organism was driving the episode.
* **Cultures are unhelpful in both directions.** Aspirates are frequently sterile; when something
  grows it is commonly skin commensals or a mixed anaerobic flora, which is what you would expect
  from any chronically broken intertriginous skin. (**consensus**) A sterile result does not stop
  the clinical picture looking like infection, and a positive result does not establish that the
  organism caused anything.
* And **it recurs**, which under the infection hypothesis reads as reinfection or inadequate
  treatment rather than as the natural history of something else.

The structural point is that every one of those observations is true, and the inference from them
is false. The inference is testable, though, because it makes predictions that fail: eradication
should end it, and does not; drainage should be curative, and is not; the organism should be
identifiable, and usually is not; and the disease should not follow a distribution determined by
where terminal follicles sit in apposed skin, which it does. A hypothesis that explains the
episode and predicts the course wrongly is the thing to notice.

## The diagnosis is clinical, and it is a triad

There is no confirmatory test, and the absence of one is the second reason for delay. The
established basis is three features together. (**consensus**, **definitional**)

| Element | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| **Typical lesions** | Deep painful nodules, abscesses, draining sinus tracts, bridged scars and double-ended ("tombstone") comedones. The bridged scar and the double-ended comedone are the two that are near-specific, because both are records of the tunnelling step |
| **Typical sites** | Intertriginous, apocrine-gland-bearing skin: axillae, groin, genital and perineal, gluteal, inframammary. Bilateral and symmetrical involvement is usual |
| **Chronicity and recurrence** | Two or more episodes over a period of months is the usual formulation. A single abscess in a typical site is not this diagnosis |

Severity staging and the several validated severity instruments exist and are in active use; their
names, cut-offs and scoring rules are in the documents named below and **none is reproduced
here**, for the same reason m089 omits the detachment thresholds.

What investigations are for: excluding the mimics and characterising complications, not confirming
the diagnosis. Swabs are for suspected superadded infection rather than for diagnosis.
(**consensus**) Imaging — ultrasound in particular — maps tract anatomy and is used where the
surgical question is how extensive the tunnelling is. (**country-dependent**, on availability.)

## One mechanism, four diseases, four sites

The follicular occlusion family is the clearest argument that the mechanism is the unit of
classification and the site is not. Four conditions, named separately and taught separately, share
the follicular occlusion-and-rupture step. (**consensus**)

| Condition | Where | What is the same |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hidradenitis suppurativa** | Axillae, groin, perineum, gluteal, inframammary | Occlusion, rupture, foreign-body response, tunnelling, fibrosis |
| **Acne conglobata** | Face, trunk, back | The same sequence in sebaceous-rich skin, with interconnecting abscesses and cysts |
| **Dissecting cellulitis of the scalp** | Scalp | The same sequence in scalp follicles, producing boggy nodules, sinuses and scarring hair loss |
| **Pilonidal sinus** | Natal cleft | The same sequence with hair as the foreign material, and a tract to show for it |

The first three are the classic triad and adding pilonidal sinus gives the tetrad.
(**definitional**) They co-occur more often than chance in individuals and in families, which is
the observation that produced the grouping in the first place.

## Associations, which are not incidental

Each of these does mechanistic work and is not a list to memorise. (**consensus**)

**Smoking** is the most consistently associated modifiable factor and is associated with severity
as well as with occurrence. **Mechanical friction, shear and occlusion in apposed skin** is why
the distribution is what it is, and is part of why body habitus matters. **Metabolic syndrome** is
over-represented and carries its own implications for cardiovascular risk assessment, which
belongs in the review rather than being left to dermatology. **Inflammatory bowel disease**,
Crohn's disease particularly, is over-represented and is also the most important mimic, so the
association and the differential point at the same thing. **Spondyloarthropathy** occurs and is
the reason to ask about inflammatory joint pain. **Polycystic ovary syndrome** is
over-represented. And a **family history** is present in a substantial minority, with autosomal
dominant inheritance in some families associated with variants in genes encoding components of the
γ-secretase complex — which is the clearest evidence available that this is a follicular disease
rather than an infective one. (**consensus**)

## What the mechanism changes about the plan

The mechanism splits the problem in two, and the split is the single most useful thing to carry
out of this answer. (**mechanism**)

**The inflammation is a process. The tunnel is an anatomical structure.** Medical treatment acts
on the first and cannot act on the second: an epithelialised sinus tract does not close because
inflammation is suppressed, any more than a fistula does. Surgical intervention — deroofing of
tracts, or wider excision of a region — acts on the second and does not alter the disease's
tendency to produce new lesions elsewhere. (**consensus**)

The consequence is that medical and surgical treatment are **not alternatives and not a sequence
of last resorts**. They address different objects. A service that offers only one of them will
produce a predictable pattern of failure, and recognising that pattern is part of the assessment.

Three further principles, at the level of principle only; no agent, dose, strength or regimen
appears in this answer.

* **Incision and drainage of an acute painful nodule** relieves the episode and is not definitive
  treatment; it does not prevent recurrence and repeated drainage at one site contributes to
  scarring. (**consensus**)
* **Modifiable factors are treatment, not advice.** Smoking cessation support and weight
  management where relevant change the course, and offering them as a referral pathway rather than
  as a remark is the difference between an intervention and a comment. (**consensus**)
* **Systemic immunomodulatory therapy, including biologic agents, has changed what is achievable**
  in moderate and severe disease over the last decade. Which agents are licensed, funded and
  reachable, and at what point in a pathway, is firmly (**country-dependent**).

One complication deserves naming separately because it is missed: **squamous cell carcinoma can
arise in long-standing disease**, particularly in gluteal and perineal sites, and a non-healing,
changing or indurated area in chronically affected skin needs biopsy rather than another course of
treatment. (**consensus**) That is a hard-won point and it is the reason a chronic diagnosis does
not retire the question of what a new lesion is.

## The mimics, in the order they matter

**Crohn's disease of the perineum** is first, because it can be clinically indistinguishable, the
two coexist, and the treatment pathways diverge. **Furunculosis** is the genuine infective
differential and is distinguished by course rather than by appearance. **Actinomycosis**,
**cutaneous tuberculosis** and **lymphogranuloma venereum** are the uncommon infective mimics,
each of which needs to be thought of to be found. And **squamous cell carcinoma**, as above, is
the one that changes everything.

## The human stakes, said plainly

No analogy in this section, and this is the section that matters most in this answer.

This is a painful disease. The pain is a primary symptom and not a complication of it, it is
frequently reported as the worst feature, and analgesic needs in severe disease are substantial
and are a specialist matter. Discharge and malodour are common, intrusive, and a major driver of
avoidance. Dressings may be needed continuously. Involvement of the groin, perineum and genital
skin affects sitting, walking, working and sexual relationships, and it is routinely not asked
about. Depression and anxiety are substantially over-represented, and so is the experience of
having been told repeatedly that the problem is hygiene — which it is not, and that correction is
itself worth making explicitly.

Visible disfigurement from scarring is permanent and the scars are in places people cannot easily
conceal in a changing room, a clinical examination or an intimate relationship. None of that is a
side issue to the mechanism; it is the reason the mechanism is worth getting right, because years
of being treated for recurrent infection is years of this.

Two things follow for anyone writing or revising. First, this is not a condition where the burden
can be inferred from the extent of the skin involved; it is reported as high at every level of
severity. Second, the specialist services and patient organisations that exist for it hold
expertise about living with it that no mechanism answer contains, and pointing someone toward them
is a clinical act.

Without softening: nothing here is for any reader's own use, and no treatment named or implied is
a recommendation. Anyone with recurrent painful lumps in these sites needs assessment in person,
and earlier rather than later, because the tunnelling and scarring steps are the irreversible
ones. Spreading redness with fever, rapidly increasing pain, or feeling systemically unwell needs
urgent assessment — call your local urgent care route or emergency number.

## Scope and safety

Revision material for someone already training in or qualified for the field. Not a clinical
reference, not a decision aid, and not reviewed by a clinician. It is not for use in making a
decision about anyone's care, including your own.

**No agent, dose, regimen, severity score, stage definition or analgesic is named in this answer,
and none of it addresses any reader's own treatment.** The omission of the staging systems and
severity instruments is deliberate: they are defined in the documents named above, and quoting a
cut-off here without the document would be worse than leaving it out. Pain management in this
condition is a specialist matter and nothing here speaks to it.

Anyone with recurrent painful lumps in the axillae, groin, perineum, buttocks or under the breasts
needs to be assessed in person, and sooner rather than later because the scarring and tunnelling
are irreversible. A non-healing or changing area within long-standing disease needs assessment and
usually biopsy. Spreading redness with fever, rapidly worsening pain, or feeling systemically
unwell is an emergency — call your local emergency number or use your local urgent care route.

Local guidance and the policy where you practise are the authority on all of this, and they differ
by country and by institution.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The mechanism is settled and is the part worth memorising: occlusion, rupture, foreign-body
response, tunnelling. The clinical triad is settled and remains the diagnosis. The
reclassification of this disease away from infection and toward chronic follicular inflammation is
also settled in specialist practice, and lags in general practice and in general surgery — which
is where the diagnostic delay accumulates, and the main reason it is worth writing down.

What moves is treatment, and it has moved more than any other part of this answer. Biologic
therapy targeting tumour necrosis factor changed what was achievable in moderate-to-severe
disease, and agents targeting other cytokine pathways have reached licensing in several
jurisdictions more recently; which are available and funded where you practise is firmly
(**country-dependent**) and is the fastest-changing thing here. Surgical practice has moved toward
tissue-sparing deroofing of tracts, with wide excision reserved rather than routine, and the
evidence base for the comparison is still developing. The nomenclature is also unsettled: "acne
inversa" has advocates and the argument is about which structure the name should point at.
Severity instruments have proliferated and harmonising them is ongoing work. Current as of October
2026.
