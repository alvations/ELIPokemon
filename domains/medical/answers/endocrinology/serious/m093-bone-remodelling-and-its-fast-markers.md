# A marker reports how fast the site is being turned over. It does not report which way the balance is running

Bone is replaced continuously, in discrete packets, by teams of cells that work in a fixed order.
A remodelling unit is activated; osteoclasts excavate a cavity over a period measured in weeks;
there is a reversal phase; osteoblasts refill the cavity over a period measured in months; and the
new matrix then mineralises over a longer period still (**mechanism**). Formation always lags
resorption, inside every single unit, by design.

Two separate things can therefore go wrong, and conflating them is the commonest error on this
topic. The **balance** within a unit can be negative, so each completed cycle leaves slightly less
bone than it found. Or the **rate** can be high, so that a larger number of units are open at any
one moment. These have different causes, different consequences and different time courses, and a
turnover marker measures the second one (**mechanism**).

## Coupling, which is why resorption and formation are not two levers

The two cell populations are not independent. Osteoblast-lineage cells control osteoclast
formation through a receptor-activator ligand and its soluble decoy receptor, and resorption in
turn liberates factors from the matrix that recruit formation (**mechanism**). Remodelling is one
process with two phases, not two processes that happen to be adjacent.

The practical consequence is the one students are most often surprised by: **a drug that
suppresses resorption also lowers formation markers**, usually after a delay, and that is the
expected behaviour of a coupled system rather than a treatment failure (**consensus**). A reader
who expects an antiresorptive agent to raise formation markers has assumed two levers where there
is one.

The same coupling explains the shape of the early density response to an antiresorptive agent.
Some of the early gain is new bone, and some of it is two other things entirely: the remodelling
space closing, because fewer cavities are open, and secondary mineralisation completing in matrix
that was already laid down (**consensus**). A density gain in the first part of treatment is
therefore not a simple measure of bone built.

## Why the pattern of a signal matters as much as its amount

Parathyroid hormone is the cleanest illustration in physiology that a hormone's effect can depend
on its **time pattern** rather than its level. Continuous elevation drives net resorption.
Intermittent exposure drives net formation (**consensus**). Same hormone, same receptor, opposite
direction, and the variable that changed was the shape of the exposure.

That is the same principle the growth-hormone answer is built on and the same one the reproductive
axis depends on, and in this specialty it is worth stating as a general rule: for several
endocrine systems the pattern is part of the signal, so a measurement that integrates the pattern
away has thrown information out. The calcium answer handles the serum calcium loop itself; what is
being described here is what the hormone does to the tissue.

Three other regulators are worth holding. Oestrogen restrains the rate of remodelling, so its
withdrawal raises turnover, which is why the menopausal transition produces both a balance problem
and a rate problem at once (**consensus**). Glucocorticoid in excess suppresses formation directly
as well as acting through other routes, which is why its bone effect does not look like the
high-turnover picture (**consensus**). And calcium and vitamin D are substrate rather than
regulators of the balance: without them the mineralisation phase cannot complete, however well the
rest of the cycle runs (**mechanism**).

## What the markers cannot tell you

Five limits, and all five are structural rather than matters of assay quality.

**They report rate, not balance.** A high resorption marker is compatible with a system in which
formation is keeping pace and with one in which it is not. The marker has no way of reporting the
difference, because it is counting units in one phase and not comparing phases (**mechanism**).

**They have no location.** Both are whole-skeleton sums, and the skeleton is not uniform —
trabecular and cortical bone turn over at different rates and respond differently (**mechanism**).
A marker cannot say which site is losing.

**Their within-person variability is large and mostly non-biological in origin.** Time of day,
whether the person has eaten, recent fracture, renal clearance of the fragment and season all move
them (**consensus**). That is why they are used as a **change against a person's own earlier
value**, under standardised sampling conditions, rather than read against a population interval,
and why the sampling conditions are specified by the laboratory (**country-dependent**).

**They are not comparable between assays or between laboratories.** A value from one platform
cannot be set beside a value from another. Which markers are offered at all differs by country
(**country-dependent**).

**They are not a density measurement and not a fracture prediction.** How much independent
information a marker adds to a risk assessment at the level of an individual is a genuinely
contested question and the documents differ; an answer that asserts one position here has
overstated the evidence (**country-dependent**).

## And the slow measurement has its own structural problems

The common density measurement is **areal**: it reports mineral per unit of projected area, which
is a two-dimensional shadow of a three-dimensional quantity (**definitional**). Two consequences
follow directly. A larger bone projects more mineral into the same outline and therefore reads
higher for reasons of geometry rather than of material (**mechanism**). And anything that adds
dense material inside the measured outline — degenerative change in the spine, a vertebral
deformity, aortic calcification, surgical metalwork — raises the reading without raising the bone
(**consensus**).

The reported score is also **relative to a chosen reference population**, which means the choice
of reference is part of the number rather than external to it (**definitional**). A reading is
therefore not interpretable without knowing which reference was used and at which site, and the
thresholds applied to it differ between countries (**country-dependent**).

Body size interacts with the measurement for the same geometric reason, and the relationship
between body weight and fracture is site-dependent rather than uniform — some sites behave
differently from others, and the areal measurement is partly confounded by overlying tissue
thickness (**consensus**). This is a measurement property, stated as such.

## The human stakes, said plainly

Most bone loss produces no symptoms at all until a fracture, which means almost everything done
about it is done on the basis of a number rather than on the basis of how someone feels. That
places real weight on understanding what the number is, and it is also why someone can be told
their reading has improved while they are no better off, and told it has not moved while treatment
is working exactly as intended.

The commonest harm in this area is not a drug side effect. It is a fracture that happened to
someone whose risk was never assessed, often after a first fracture that was treated as an injury
and not as information. The second commonest is the mirror image: a person started on long-term
treatment on the strength of a single reading that was raised or lowered by one of the measurement
artefacts above.

A note about who is reading. Someone reading this may have had a density scan, or be taking
something for their bones. Nothing here is a threshold, a target, a duration or a plan. What a
particular reading means, whether treatment is working, and how long it should continue are
judgements made against a whole picture by the team that holds it, and the plan a person already
has is the one that applies.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The remodelling cycle and its phase order, the coupling of the two cell populations, the
remodelling-space argument, the dependence of the parathyroid-hormone effect on its time pattern,
and the geometric limits of an areal measurement are mechanism and do not date. What moves is
everything procedural and numeric: which markers are recommended and under what sampling
conditions, the reference populations and thresholds applied to density readings, the risk
calculators in use, treatment durations and the questions around interrupting treatment. All of
those are country-dependent and under active revision. Check current local guidance and your
laboratory's handbook.
