# Stored energy is a controlled variable with its own sensor, its own reporter and a defence that is far stronger in one direction than the other

This answer is about a control system. It is not about people, it contains no advice, and it
contains no numbers — the reasons for the last of those are in the scope section and they are not
incidental.

The central claim is a physiological one and it is settled: **body fat mass is a regulated
variable, not a passive accumulation** (**consensus**). There is a sensor — adipose tissue reports
its own mass hormonally. There is a controller — hypothalamic circuits that integrate that report
with short-term signals from the gut. And there are effectors on both sides of the balance, acting
on intake and on energy expenditure. That is a closed loop with all the parts a loop needs, and
treating stored energy as the output of a loop rather than as a running total is what the rest of
this answer is about (**mechanism**).

The single most consequential property of the loop is that **its defence is asymmetrical**. It
opposes loss strongly, through several mechanisms at once, and it opposes gain weakly
(**consensus**). A thermostat is a poor analogy for that, because a thermostat is symmetrical. The
behaviour is closer to a defended floor with a permissive ceiling, and the floor can move upward
and then be defended at the new position.

This answer borrows one argument wholesale. m021 distinguishes two ways a hormonal loop can break
— the signal absent, or the loop running with the gain turned down — and the second of those,
m021's section on type 2 diabetes, is the structure of leptin resistance exactly. It is applied
below and not re-derived.

Claims are marked (**mechanism**) where they follow from physiology and are checkable by
reasoning, (**definitional**) where the statement is what a term means, (**consensus**) where they
are settled professional agreement, and (**country-dependent**) where the answer differs between
countries and documents. Where a question is genuinely **unsettled** this answer says so in those
words rather than reaching for a marker it does not deserve.

## The loop, and the two clocks running through it

```
   THE STORE REPORTS ITS OWN SIZE

   ADIPOSE TISSUE ── secretes a hormone broadly in proportion to its mass
        │                                         │
        │   LONG CLOCK: days and weeks.           │  SHORT CLOCK: one meal.
        │   Reports the SIZE OF THE STORE.        │  Reports what is
        │                                         │  happening right now.
        │                                         │
        │                              GUT ── a pre-meal orexigenic signal
        │                                  ── post-meal satiety signals
        │                                  ── vagal afferents: distension
        │                                         │
        └──────────────┬──────────────────────────┘
                       ▼
             HYPOTHALAMIC CIRCUITS
             two opposing neuronal populations in the same nucleus:
               ├── one reduces intake
               └── one increases it
             Both read the store signal. Both read the meal signals.
                       │
          ┌────────────┴────────────┐
          ▼                         ▼
      INTAKE                   ENERGY EXPENDITURE
      hunger, satiety,         resting rate, the thermic effect of
      food reward              food, spontaneous activity
          │                         │
          └────────────┬────────────┘
                       ▼
             CHANGE IN THE STORE ──────────► back to the top

   ── AND THE ASYMMETRY, WHICH IS THE WHOLE POINT ──

   store FALLS   ──►  expenditure falls MORE than the lost tissue predicts
                 ──►  orexigenic signalling rises
                 ──►  satiety signalling falls
                 ──►  and all of that PERSISTS beyond the period of loss
                      ═══ a strong, multi-channel, sustained defence ═══

   store RISES   ──►  a much weaker opposition
                      ═══ and the defended position can move UP ═══
```

Two features of that diagram carry most of the clinical content. The store signal and the meal
signals **converge on the same circuits**, which is why a long-term regulator and a short-term one
are not two separate systems to be managed separately (**mechanism**). And the effector arms
include **energy expenditure**, not only intake — which is why the loop can defend its position
without any change in behaviour at all (**mechanism**).

## The defence is asymmetrical, and that is the clinical problem

Reduce the store and the system responds on several channels simultaneously (**consensus**):

* **Energy expenditure falls by more than the loss of tissue accounts for.** A smaller body needs
  less energy, and that part is arithmetic. The observed fall is larger than the arithmetic
  predicts, which is a regulatory response rather than a consequence of size.
* **Signalling shifts towards intake.** Hunger signalling rises and satiety signalling falls, in
  the same direction, at the same time.
* **It persists.** The response does not resolve when the loss stops. It is measurable long after.

Increase the store and the opposition is comparatively weak. There is some — energy expenditure
rises with a larger body, and satiety signalling rises — but nothing like the coordinated,
sustained, multi-channel response that loss produces (**consensus**).

Two consequences follow directly and both are mechanical.

**Weight regain after loss is the expected behaviour of the system, not a failure of the person in
it.** A loop that defends one direction strongly will return towards its defended position when
the external force is removed. Predicting otherwise would require the loop not to exist.

**The asymmetry is a ratchet rather than a spring.** A spring returns to one position from either
side. This system returns from below and tolerates movement above, and the defended position can
shift upward and then be defended at the new level (**consensus**). **By what mechanisms the
defended position moves, and how reversibly, is not settled**, and this answer will not pretend
otherwise.

## Signal high, response low — which is m021's second break

The obvious prediction from the diagram is that a large store means a large signal means
suppressed intake. In common obesity the first two hold and the third does not: the circulating
store hormone is **high**, not low (**consensus**).

So the loop is not broken by an absent signal. It is running, reporting correctly, and the
response to the report is reduced — which is exactly the second of m021's two breaks, the one that
answer describes as the loop still running with the gain turned down. The term in general use for
this state is leptin resistance (**definitional**).

The decisive evidence for where the defect sits is therapeutic and it cuts both ways
(**consensus**):

* In the **rare** congenital deficiency of the store hormone, there is no signal at all,
  replacement supplies it, and the response is dramatic. Signal absent, receiver intact.
* In **common** obesity, the signal is already high, giving more of it does not produce a
  corresponding response, and that is not a dosing problem.

A loop whose receiver has stopped responding cannot be fixed by increasing the thing it has
stopped responding to. That is the general principle and it is worth stating separately from this
disease, because it recurs: m057's replacement that does not close the loop and m056's exogenous
supply are the same shape of argument from the other end.

## Why a measured number is a poor instrument for the regulated quantity

The regulated quantity is stored energy, distributed in particular places. The number usually
recorded is a ratio of mass to height squared, and the gap between those two things is not a
technicality.

**It cannot decompose what it is made of.** The same value can arise from different combinations
of fat mass, lean mass and skeletal frame, and nothing in the number says which (**mechanism**).
It is a population instrument — it performs reasonably as a descriptor of a group and poorly as a
description of an individual, which is a general property of that kind of measure and not a defect
specific to this one.

**Where the store sits matters more than its total for metabolic risk.** Visceral adipose tissue
and fat deposited ectopically in liver and muscle are associated with metabolic consequences far
more closely than total mass is, and two people with the same total can differ substantially in
distribution (**consensus**). This is the mechanism behind the clinical habit of recording a waist
measurement alongside, or instead of, the ratio.

**Its relationship to risk is not the same in every population.** Several countries and bodies
apply different cut-offs for some populations on exactly that basis, and which apply where you
work is a local question (**country-dependent**). No cut-offs from any system appear in this
answer.

**And adipose tissue is not inert storage.** It secretes hormones and inflammatory mediators, and
its secretory profile changes with its mass and its location, which is why it is described as an
endocrine organ and why the metabolic consequences are not simply a mechanical function of load
(**consensus**).

## What acting on the regulator looks like

If the quantity is regulated, then an intervention has two possible targets: the regulated
variable, or the regulator. Those are different acts with different durability, and the
distinction is mechanistic rather than moral.

**Acting against the regulator produces change that the regulator opposes.** Reducing intake moves
the variable, and the loop responds on every channel described above, including the ones that are
not behavioural. The change is real while the force is applied and is opposed for as long as it is
applied and afterwards (**mechanism**). That is a statement about a control system. It is not a
statement about anybody's effort, resolve or character, and it is the single most important thing
in this answer to get the register right on.

**Acting on the regulator changes what is being defended.** Two classes of intervention are
understood to do this, and both are described here as mechanisms with **no agents, doses,
eligibility criteria or outcome figures**:

* **Pharmacotherapy acting on the gut-hormone signalling arms** of the loop, which engages the
  satiety side of the same circuits the diagram above draws (**consensus**).
* **Metabolic and bariatric surgery**, where the mechanistic understanding has moved
  substantially. A purely restrictive explanation — a smaller volume, therefore less intake — is
  now considered incomplete, and changes in gut hormone signalling and in the defended level are
  understood to carry a large part of the effect (**consensus**). Which procedures are offered, to
  whom, and on what criteria differ markedly between countries and health systems
  (**country-dependent**).

The generalisable point is the one that the whole answer exists to establish: **in a regulated
system, durable change requires acting on the regulation.** That is true of blood pressure, of
glucose and of bone density, and it is true here. Nothing about the statement is specific to this
variable, and that is why it is worth making this way.

## What this answer is not saying

Four boundaries, because this is a subject where an unstated boundary gets filled in by the
reader.

**It is not saying that intake and activity are irrelevant.** They are the effector arms of the
loop. The claim is about where durable leverage sits, not about what the inputs are.

**It is not saying that the defended position is fixed or that change is impossible.** The
position moves — that is why it can move upward — and interventions that act on regulation exist.

**It is not taking a position on whether obesity should be classified as a disease.** Bodies
differ on this and the classification carries consequences for funding, access and language that
are not physiological (**country-dependent**).

**And it is not a complete account.** The mechanisms by which the defended position moves upward,
the respective contributions of the food environment, sleep, the gut microbiome, early life and
genetics, and how reversible any of it is, are **unsettled** and are where the primary literature
should be read rather than a revision page.

## The human stakes, said plainly

This is the subject in the specialty where the register of the writing is itself part of the harm
or part of the remedy, so it is worth saying what this page has tried to do and why.

People with obesity are routinely treated, in clinical settings, as though the condition were
evidence about their character. That is a well-documented phenomenon with measurable consequences:
it is associated with avoidance of health care, with delayed presentation of unrelated conditions,
and with symptoms being attributed to weight without being investigated. A person who has been
weighed and lectured at every appointment for twenty years has a rational basis for not attending
the next one. The clinical cost of that is borne by the person and is caused by the service.

The mechanism described above matters here because it is, among other things, a correction. A loop
that defends one direction strongly will return towards its defended position when the applied
force stops. That is the expected behaviour of the system. It is recorded in notes as failure,
relapse, and non-adherence — three words that describe a person and not a loop.

Two further things that are not mechanism.

Weight stigma is not confined to clinicians. It is in the equipment, in the furniture, in the
gowns, in the cuffs, and in the width of a chair in a waiting room, and the absence of a thing
that fits is a message whether anybody intended one.

And the language used about this condition is contested by the people who have it, with reasonable
disagreement about which terms are acceptable and to whom. Asking rather than assuming is the only
general rule available.

A note about who is reading. Anybody reading about this is more likely to be living with it than
revising it. If that is you: there are deliberately no numbers, cut-offs, categories, drugs or
diets anywhere on this page, and nothing here is advice about your body. What the mechanism above
does say is that the thing being described is a regulated physiological system and not a verdict
about you. Anything about what to do belongs with your own clinical team, who can also be asked to
investigate a symptom on its merits.

## Scope and safety

This is revision material about a control system, written for someone training in or qualified for
the field. It is not a clinical reference, not a decision aid, not advice about anybody's weight,
and not about any individual's care. **No body-mass-index values, cut-offs, waist measurements,
categories, drug names, doses, eligibility criteria or outcome figures appear here on purpose.**
This is the subject where a number lifted from a revision page is most likely to be read as a
verdict about the reader, and the physiology is explicable without any of them. Check your own
national guidance and your national formulary. Nothing here has had clinical review. A symptom
deserves investigation on its merits regardless of anybody's weight, and that is a clinical matter
rather than a reading one.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The existence of the loop, its sensor and its two clocks, the asymmetry of its defence, the
persistence of the counter-regulatory response, the location of the defect in common obesity on
the response side rather than the signal side, and the general principle that durable change in a
regulated variable requires acting on its regulation are mechanism and settled consensus, and they
do not date. What moves is substantial: the mechanisms by which the defended position shifts, the
relative contributions of environment, sleep, microbiome, early life and genetics, the
pharmacology of this area — which has changed faster than any other part of this specialty in
recent years — the mechanistic account of metabolic surgery, which measures and cut-offs are
recommended and to which populations, commissioning and eligibility criteria, the
disease-classification question, and the guidance on language and on stigma. All of those are
country-dependent, revised, or openly unsettled. Check current local guidance, your national
formulary and the primary literature.
