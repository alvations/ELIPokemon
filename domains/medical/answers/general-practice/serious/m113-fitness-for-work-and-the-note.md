# Fitness for work is a property of a person and a job together, and neither half is a measurement

There is no investigation that returns a verdict on whether somebody can work, and there is no
prospect of one, because the question is not about the person alone. It is a comparison between
what this person can currently do and what this particular job currently requires, under whatever
adjustments are actually available. Change any of the three terms and the answer changes without
anything about the illness having changed at all (**mechanism**). That is why the judgement
resists measurement: it is a relation, and a test can only measure one of the things being
related.

Load-bearing claims below are marked with what they rest on: **mechanism**, **definitional**,
**consensus** or **country-dependent**. A claim marked **mechanism** is checkable by reasoning. A
claim marked **country-dependent** needs the reader's own guidance, not this answer.

The name of the document, its legal force, who may issue it, after how long it becomes required,
whether it is advice or instruction, and whether it can recommend adjustments rather than absence
differ substantially between countries and are revised (**country-dependent**). The reasoning
below is about the judgement, not about any one country's form.

## Why no test can answer it

Four reasons, and they are different from each other:

1. **The outcome is not a biological state.** Capacity for work is defined relative to a task,
   which makes it definitionally not a property of the person (**definitional**). A measurement of
   the person cannot be a measurement of a relation.
2. **The relevant function is often unobservable in a consultation.** Sustained attention over a
   shift, tolerance of repetition, the fourth hour rather than the first, and the effect of a
   commute before the work starts are none of them things a ten-minute encounter samples.
3. **Variability is the clinical feature.** For many conditions what matters is not the mean but
   the worst day and the unpredictability, and a single measurement is a single draw from a
   distribution.
4. **The question is partly about risk to others**, in some roles, which is a different question
   with a different threshold and, in many systems, a different decision-maker
   (**country-dependent**).

## What the note is actually doing, which is four things at once

* **Clinical advice.** The part that is a clinical act: a judgement about function, for a stated
  period, with a stated expectation of change.
* **A trigger for income protection.** In most systems it is the mechanism by which somebody is
  paid, or not, while unable to work. This is why the document is not optional and why its absence
  is a financial event rather than an administrative one (**country-dependent**).
* **Evidence in an employment process.** It enters a file, is read by people who are not
  clinicians, and may be used in decisions about the person's job.
* **A record entry.** It persists, is inherited by every subsequent clinician, and shapes the
  prior applied to the next presentation — m084's point about labelling applies to certification
  as directly as to diagnosis.

Those four pull in different directions, which is the main reason the task feels harder than its
clinical content. The clinical judgement is usually not the difficult part.

## The commonest errors, named

* **Certifying the diagnosis instead of the function.** A diagnosis on the form tells the reader
  nothing about what the person can do, and it discloses more than the task requires.
* **All-or-nothing certification.** Treating the choice as *work* or *no work* when a reduced,
  adjusted or phased return is available discards the term with the most leverage in the whole
  judgement (**consensus**, where such a route exists locally; **country-dependent** whether it
  does).
* **Letting the appointment interval set the duration.** The period should follow the expected
  clinical course. When it follows the next free appointment it is an administrative artefact
  wearing a clinical number.
* **Writing what was requested without naming it as advice.** Recording the person's own account
  of their work capacity is legitimate and useful; presenting it as an independent clinical
  assessment is not, and the distinction belongs in the record.
* **Treating the request as suspect by default.** The base rate assumption that a request is
  illegitimate is both usually wrong and corrosive to the consultation, and it falls hardest on
  the people m050 describes.
* **Certifying without ever asking what the job is.** Column two is the largest term and the
  cheapest to obtain, and it is the one most often skipped.

## Where the position is genuinely difficult

The clinician is in two roles at once: the person's clinician, and the assessor whose signature a
third party relies on. That is a real conflict and not a failure of character — the first role is
built on believing an account that cannot be verified, and the second is relied on precisely
because it is supposed to be independent of the person's wishes (**mechanism**). Most systems
resolve it imperfectly by keeping the document advisory, so that the employer rather than the
clinician makes the employment decision, and by routing contested or complex cases to
occupational health, which is a different assessor with a different relationship
(**country-dependent**).

The honest position is that the judgement is made on partial information, that the person's
account is the main instrument, and that saying so in the record is better than implying a
precision the assessment does not have.

## The human stakes, said plainly

The consequences of this judgement are mostly financial and they are immediate. For many people
the note is the difference between being paid and not, and the gap between a clinician's
timescales and a household's is where the harm lives. A duration chosen for clinical tidiness can
mean weeks without income. That is a real harm, caused by the document rather than by the illness,
and it is not visible from inside the consultation.

It runs the other way too. Certification that is too easily extended can be a slow harm: long
absence is associated with a reduced chance of returning at all, and the longer it runs the more
the loss is of work, income, routine, social contact and a part of how somebody sees themselves.
The direction of that association is mainstream; its magnitude and how much of it is causal are
argued about, and it does not license pressing anybody back into work that is unsafe or harmful
(**consensus** on direction; magnitude contested). Good work, on average, supports health better
than worklessness. Bad work does not, and the distinction matters more than the generalisation.

And the thing that is hardest to carry. People asking for this document are frequently frightened
— of losing their job, of not being believed, of what will happen if the answer is no — and the
question arrives inside a consultation that may have been about something else entirely. Being
disbelieved while unwell is its own injury, and it is distributed unequally: it falls most on
people whose symptoms are not visible, whose conditions fluctuate, whose first language is not the
system's, and who have been doubted before. The clinical reasoning above does not touch any of
that. It is the part of the task that cannot be made into a structure, and it is the part that the
person will remember.

## What an examiner digs into next

Whether the candidate states the judgement as a relation rather than a property, unprompted. Then
the three columns, with the observation that two of them are not clinical facts and one of them
the clinician usually cannot see. Then why the diagnosis is a weak predictor, and what replaces
it. Then the four simultaneous functions of the document, and which of them creates the pressure
in the room. Then the errors, with the appointment-interval artefact recognised. Then the role
conflict, named as structural rather than personal. Then the hardest one — what they would record
when the account is the only evidence and they are not in a position to assess it.

## Scope and safety

This is revision material about the structure of a fitness-for-work judgement, written for someone
already training in or qualified for the field. It is not a clinical reference, not a decision
aid, and nothing here should inform whether any individual is certified, for how long, or on what
terms — that belongs with the clinician assessing them and with the national scheme in force. No
duration, threshold, criterion or form wording is named here on purpose: all of them are
country-specific and all are revised. **"Capacity for work" in this answer means functional
ability against job demands, and is not mental capacity** — decision-making capacity, consent and
the law around them are a separate subject, are deliberately not treated here, and are governed
by their own local legislation. If someone is unwell right now, the relevant action is to contact
local urgent care or the local emergency number, not to read this.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The structural claims — that fitness for work is a relation between function, task and
adjustment, that no test can measure a relation, and that the diagnosis is the weakest of the
available predictors — are mechanism and will not move. Everything institutional will and does:
the name and format of the document, who may issue it, the waiting period before it is required,
whether it can recommend adjustments, how it interacts with income protection, and the extent to
which issuing is being extended to professions other than doctors. Several countries have changed
more than one of those in the last decade and the direction is not uniform. The evidence base on
work and health has grown and remains contested in magnitude. Take the scheme itself from current
national guidance rather than from here, as of October 2026.
