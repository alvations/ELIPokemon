# Coverage is a product of terms, and the record only stores the result

A vaccination programme does not have one failure mode. It has a chain of them, and coverage is
what survives all of them at once: the eligible person has to be identifiable, the offer has to
reach them, the offer has to be reachable, the encounter has to happen, the product has to be
present and in condition, and the event has to be recorded. Each of those is a separate term, the
terms multiply rather than add, and the measured output — a person who is not vaccinated — is
identical whichever term failed (**mechanism**).

That single sentence is the whole of why *confidence* and *access* have to be held apart. They are
two different terms in the same product. They are distributed differently across a population,
they are moved by different interventions, and they are indistinguishable in the data a practice
holds.

Load-bearing claims below are marked with what they rest on: **mechanism**, **definitional**,
(**consensus**) or (**country-dependent**). No schedule, interval, age, coverage figure or
threshold for population protection appears here, deliberately: every one of them is set
nationally, several are revised annually, and an invented one would be the most damaging thing
this answer could contain. This answer is about the structure of the system, not about any
vaccine.

Terms 1 to 3 and 5 to 6 are *access and system* terms. Term 4 is the *confidence* term. The reason
the distinction is not pedantry is that the two groups respond to completely different things, and
that nothing in routine data says which group a given uncounted person belongs to (**mechanism**).

## Why the two terms are genuinely different in kind

**Access terms are properties of the service.** They are set by opening hours, location, booking
route, cost at the point of use, language of the invitation, registration requirements, and
whether somebody has a usual clinician at all. m082 is the full argument: a door that is hard to
open reduces recorded demand and does not reduce need, and the attributes it filters on —
literacy, flexible employment, transport, confidence in operating a system — are distributed in
the same direction as need (**consensus**). Every one of those terms is auditable from inside the
practice, and every one of them is changeable by a decision somebody in the service is authorised
to make.

**The decision term is not a property of the service, and it is not one attribute.** It varies by
vaccine rather than by person — the same household may accept one offer without hesitation and
have an unresolved question about another. It is reached once per offer, not once per lifetime.
And the reasons behind it are heterogeneous to the point where treating them as a single quantity
is itself the error: an unanswered question, a prior experience of care, an incomplete
understanding of what the offer is for, and a considered decision to decline are different states
that produce the same entry in the record (**mechanism**).

This answer does not characterise anybody's reasons, and that is a deliberate limit rather than an
omission. The clinically useful move is the opposite of a generalisation: find out which term
failed for the person in front of you, because the four states above lead to four different
conversations and only one of them is a declination (**consensus**).

## What the population mechanism adds, and what it does not

Vaccination has an effect no individual intervention has: when a sufficient proportion of a
population is immune, transmission chains break more often than they propagate, and people who are
not immune are protected by that without receiving anything (**mechanism**, **consensus**). Four
consequences follow, and the third is the one that changes practice.

1. **The distribution matters as much as the average.** Protection depends on contact patterns, so
   the relevant quantity is coverage *in the groups that mix*, not coverage across an area. A
   locality can sit at a comfortable-looking average and contain a cluster where transmission is
   sustained indefinitely. An average is the wrong summary of a quantity that behaves non-linearly
   in the tail.
2. **The individual benefit is small, diffuse, and mostly not attributable to the person who took
   it.** This is exactly m085's prevention paradox: a measure can prevent many cases across a
   population while offering almost nobody in it a benefit they could notice.
3. **Benefits are invisible and harms are attributable.** An averted infection has no date, no
   name and no consultation attached. An adverse event after an immunisation has all three. This
   is m084's observability asymmetry in its sharpest form, and it means professional and public
   learning both drift in one direction no matter how conscientious anybody is. The counterweights
   have to be designed: population data rather than recalled cases, and surveillance systems that
   are set up to detect and to quantify rather than only to collect reports (**consensus**).
4. **Success removes its own evidence.** As incidence falls, the disease stops being part of
   clinical experience — of the clinician's as much as anyone's — while the side-effect profile
   stays exactly as visible as it was.

## The schedule and the cold chain are mechanism, not administration

Two parts of a programme are routinely treated as logistics and are not.

**Spacing.** The timing of a series is not an administrative convenience. It is set by how the
immune system responds — when a response can be mounted at all, how long the interval has to be
for a subsequent dose to boost rather than be wasted, and how maternally derived immunity
interacts with the first doses. Shifting a schedule is therefore an immunological decision and not
a scheduling one (**mechanism**). The actual ages and intervals are set nationally, differ between
countries for defensible reasons, and are revised; they are not stated here
(**country-dependent**).

**Storage.** The product is biological and loses potency outside its storage conditions, which
makes the chain from manufacture to administration part of the clinical intervention rather than a
precondition for it. A dose given from a chain that broke may be indistinguishable at the point of
administration from one that did not, which is why storage is monitored as a control rather than
audited afterwards (**mechanism**, **consensus**). The specific conditions, excursion procedures
and reporting routes are local (**country-dependent**).

## What the consultation contributes, framed as a term rather than a technique

The encounter is one term in the product, and what it can do is bounded.

* **It can make the offer exist.** An offer not made is the commonest reason a term fails, and the
  opportunistic offer — made because somebody came in for something else — is one of the few
  things that reaches people whom the invitation route does not. m082 notes that raising the cost
  of coming in silently stops that work happening.
* **It can move the information to where the decision is.** That is m083's point about decision
  aids: the function is not to supply new facts but to put the existing ones in front of the
  person at the moment they are deciding, in a form they can hold and in both directions.
* **It can answer the question actually asked**, which requires finding out what it is. A question
  is not a declination, and recording it as one closes a loop that was still open.
* **It can record the state accurately.** A record that distinguishes *not offered*, *offer not
  reached*, *question outstanding* and *declined* lets the next clinician start from the right
  place. A blank cannot hold any of those, which is nursing m073's problem exactly: a storage
  class that fails quietly into "no".

What the consultation cannot do is substitute for the terms it does not control. If the binding
term is reachability, the conversation is not where the loss is, and the honest system response is
to change the door (**mechanism**; how much each term contributes is local and measurable only
locally).

## The human stakes, said plainly

The people least likely to be counted as vaccinated are, repeatedly and in many countries, the
people least likely to be registered, least likely to have a usual clinician, most likely to be
moving, and most likely to have had an experience of health services that gives them no reason to
expect to be listened to. That is the inverse care law operating on a preventive programme, and
m050 is the argument for why it is a mechanism rather than a complaint.

It also matters how the uncounted are described. A person who was never reached, a person who
could not get there, a person with an unanswered question and a person who has decided are four
different people, and collapsing them into one category is both inaccurate and corrosive: it
attributes to individual choice a failure that is mostly structural, and it makes the conversation
with the fourth group harder by having been conducted in public about all four.

And the asymmetry that nobody involved gets to escape. Serious adverse events after immunisation
are rare and real, and when one happens it happens to an identifiable person who was well, who
came in for prevention, and whose harm is attributable to a specific act. Infections averted are
neither identifiable nor attributable. A clinician who holds both halves honestly is holding a
genuinely uncomfortable position, and the discomfort is not a sign of having reasoned badly. The
answer is not to flatten either side: it is to say plainly that a population-level benefit and an
individual-level harm are both real, that surveillance exists because of the second, and that no
amount of arithmetic makes the second easier for the person it happened to.

## What an examiner digs into next

Whether the candidate separates the terms without being prompted, and states that they multiply.
Then which terms are properties of the service and which is not, with the audit consequence
attached. Then the readout problem: that the record stores the result and not the cause, and what
would have to be recorded for the cause to survive. Then the four states that produce one entry,
and why only one of them is a declination. Then the distribution argument — coverage in mixing
groups rather than across an area, and why an average is the wrong summary. Then the observability
asymmetry, offered unprompted, with the design of the counterweight. Then the hard one — what
their own practice could measure to tell an access failure from a decision, and what it would cost
to measure it.

## Scope and safety

This is revision material about how a vaccination programme behaves as a system, written for
someone already training in or qualified for the field. It is not a clinical reference, not a
decision aid, and nothing here should inform whether any individual receives any vaccine, when, or
in what order. **No schedule, age, interval, dose, contraindication, coverage figure or
population-protection threshold is named here on purpose** — all are set nationally, all are
revised, and the national schedule and its handbook are the only acceptable source for them.
Nothing here is a statement about the safety or effectiveness of any particular product; those
belong to the product information and to the national body that reviews it. No individual's or
group's reasons for a decision are characterised here, and the answer does not contain a method
for changing anybody's mind. If someone is unwell right now, the relevant action is to contact
local urgent care or the local emergency number, not to read this.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The structural claims — that coverage is a product of terms, that the terms multiply so the
binding one dominates, that access and the decision are different terms with different remedies,
that the record stores the result rather than the cause, that distribution matters more than the
average, and that benefits are unattributable while harms are not — are mechanism and consensus,
and they are stable. Everything specific moves: schedules, products, eligible groups, catch-up
arrangements, the channels through which offers are made, and who is authorised to administer.
Several countries have changed more than one of those in the last few years and the direction is
not uniform. The evidence base on what improves uptake has grown substantially and remains
setting-dependent. Take the schedule and everything attached to it from current national guidance
rather than from here, as of October 2026.
