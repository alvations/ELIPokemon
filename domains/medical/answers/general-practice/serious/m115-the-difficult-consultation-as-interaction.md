# "Nothing I do works" is a composite readout with several unrelated causes

The older literature on this gave the phenomenon a name taken from the clinician's side of the
desk, and the name has been widely criticised as pejorative and is avoided by many clinicians and
services; the term is not used in this answer (**consensus** that the usage is contested). What is
not contested is that the phenomenon is real, common, and worth analysing. The analytical move
that makes it tractable is to notice that the clinician's sense of futility is a *single readout*
with several structurally different generators behind it, in the same way that one presenting
complaint can have a dozen mechanisms (m101). A readout that cannot distinguish its own causes
will be attributed to the most visible candidate, and in a consulting room the most visible
candidate is the person sitting there (**mechanism**).

Load-bearing claims below are marked with what they rest on: **mechanism**, **definitional**,
**consensus** or **country-dependent**. A claim marked **mechanism** is checkable by reasoning. A
claim marked **country-dependent** needs the reader's own guidance, not this answer.

## Five generators, and they are not interchangeable

```

   different in every row, which is the whole reason the distinction is worth making.

   ┌───────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ generator                     │ what actually changes it                     │
   ├───────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┤

   TWO OF THE FIVE ARE NOT ABOUT THE OTHER PERSON AT ALL, and a third is not about
   either party. So a readout attributed to the patient will be wrong in at least
   three of the five cases — and in the fifth case the attribution is not merely
   wrong, it is the mechanism by which a diagnosis gets missed.
```

## The feeling is a datum, and it is a datum about the interaction

The clinician's response is reliable evidence that something in the encounter is not working. It
is unreliable evidence about *what*. Holding those two statements together is the whole skill: the
feeling should be attended to and not acted on directly (**mechanism**).

What it supports is a question — *what is happening here* — rather than a conclusion. Three
things make it more useful:

* **Noticing it at the time**, because it is far easier to reason about in the moment than to
  reconstruct afterwards.
* **Separating it from its object.** "I feel helpless in this consultation" and "this person is
  difficult" are different statements, and only the first one is observation.
* **Checking whether it generalises.** If a colleague finds the same consultations hard, the
  generator is more likely to be rows 1, 2, 4 or 5. If only one clinician does, row 3 is in play,
  and that is information about the clinician rather than a criticism of them.

## The diagnostic risk, which is why this is not only about comfort

Once an encounter has been categorised as difficult, and especially once a person has been
categorised, the record carries that categorisation forward and the prior applied to every
subsequent symptom shifts (**mechanism**). The failure mode is attribution: a new finding is
assigned to the existing pattern rather than assessed on its own terms. This is the same
mechanism m105 describes for a presentation that looks social, and the same labelling persistence
m084 describes for a diagnosis — the label outlives the reason for it, is inherited by every
subsequent clinician, and is not visible as a source of error to anyone downstream.

So the practical risk runs in a specific direction. The people most likely to have a new serious
diagnosis missed are those who attend often and have been categorised, which is also the group in
whom the prior probability of serious disease is, on the whole, higher rather than lower
(**consensus**). The labelling and the risk move in opposite directions, which is what makes it
dangerous.

The counterweight has to be designed rather than willed. What works is a deliberate re-opening of
the diagnostic question at stated intervals — not at every contact, which is both unsustainable
and itself iatrogenic, and not never.

## What changes the interaction, in order of how much it buys

* **Set the agenda out loud, early, and jointly.** The single highest-yield act, because row 1 is
  the commonest generator and it is invisible unless asked about. It is m048's argument about the
  opening applied to a consultation that has already gone wrong several times.
* **Agree a plan with a date and a named holder.** An episode where every contact starts again is
  row 4, and the remedy is structural: one plan, written down, with what happens next and who is
  responsible, so the next contact continues rather than restarts.
* **Continuity.** Where the problem is that nobody holds the thread, the intervention with the
  best evidence is the same one m015 describes, and it is an intervention rather than a
  preference.
* **Separate the two questions.** *What is the diagnosis* and *what is this consultation for* are
  different, and conflating them is why a consultation with no diagnostic progress feels like a
  failed consultation when it was not one.
* **Spread the load.** Case discussion, a supervision or Balint-style group, or a shared plan
  across a team moves the problem out of one person's head. Where it exists, this is also the main
  protection against row 3 (**country-dependent**, since what is available differs by system).
* **Name the limits honestly.** For row 2, telling somebody plainly that there is no test that
  will settle it and that the work is now about function is more respectful and more effective
  than repeated investigation, and it is also the hardest of these to do well.

## The label is the thing to drop

The term inherited from the older literature attaches the difficulty to a person, and that is the
error the rest of this answer is about. There are two reasons to stop using it, and the second is
the stronger:

1. **It is inaccurate.** It names a property of an encounter as a property of one participant,
   and in at least three of the five generators the participant is not the source.
2. **It is consequential.** A label in a record changes how the next clinician reads the next
   symptom, which is a patient-safety matter and not a question of manners.

What replaces it is a description: what is being sought, what is being offered, what the system is
doing to the episode, and what the clinician is bringing. That is longer, and it is the kind of
entry a colleague can actually use.

## The human stakes, said plainly

The person in the chair is usually having a worse time than the clinician. People who consult
repeatedly without resolution are frequently in distress, frequently unwell in ways that have not
been named, and frequently aware — accurately — that they are experienced as a burden. Knowing
that about yourself, in a room you came to for help, is its own harm, and it is a harm the
consultation produces rather than one it inherits.

This falls unequally. The categorisation attaches most readily to people whose symptoms are not
visible, whose conditions fluctuate, who have no diagnosis yet, whose first language is not the
system's, who are poor, or who have a psychiatric diagnosis already in the record. That is m050's
mechanism arriving as a social process inside a consultation, and better intentions do not fix it;
only the discipline of describing the interaction rather than the person does, and imperfectly.

And the clinician's side, stated without self-pity and without dismissal. Repeated consultations
in which nothing is achieved are a real occupational load, they contribute to burnout, and the
feelings that come with them — irritation, dread, guilt about the irritation — are ordinary and
not evidence of a defect of character. They are also not a private matter: a clinician carrying
them alone is more likely to make the attribution error above. The route out is collegial and
structural rather than personal, and that is the reason case discussion and supervision are
described here as interventions rather than as support.

One thing is deliberately set aside. Where the distress in the room is a mental-health crisis, or
where there is risk to the person, none of the analysis above applies and it is not the relevant
frame; the applicable local urgent mental-health pathway is the authority
(**country-dependent**).

## What an examiner digs into next

Whether the candidate relocates the difficulty into the interaction unprompted, and avoids the
inherited term. Then the five generators, with the observation that two are not about the other
person and one is about neither party. Then the formulation that the feeling is reliable about
*that* something is wrong and unreliable about *what*. Then the diagnostic risk, with the
direction of the inequality named. Then the interventions, ordered, with agenda-setting first and
continuity recognised as structural. Then the record: what they would actually write, and why the
description beats the label. Then the hardest one — how they would re-open the diagnostic question
in an episode that has had twenty contacts, without restarting from nothing and without never
looking again.

## Scope and safety

This is revision material about the structure of a consultation, written for someone already
training in or qualified for the field. It is not a clinical reference, not a decision aid, and
nothing here should inform the assessment or management of any individual — that belongs with the
clinician who knows them. Nothing here is a description of any real person or consultation; every
scenario is constructed. This answer is not about mental-health crisis, risk of self-harm or
safeguarding, and the reasoning in it does not apply to any of those: where any of them is in
question the local urgent pathway or safeguarding procedure is the authority and takes precedence.
No diagnostic label, criterion or attendance threshold is named here on purpose. If someone is
unwell or in crisis right now, the relevant action is to contact local urgent care, the local
urgent mental-health service or the local emergency number, not to read this.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The structural claim — that the readout has several unrelated generators and is therefore a poor
guide to its own cause — is mechanism and will not move. The framing around it has moved a great
deal and continues to: the older clinician-centred terminology has been substantially abandoned
in teaching and in published guidance over the last two decades, the management framing for
persistent physical symptoms has been revised more than once and differs between countries, and
the literature on diagnostic error and attribution has grown rapidly. Terminology in this area is
itself contested, so a reader should expect the words to differ from the words used here and
should take them from current local sources rather than from this answer, as of October 2026.
