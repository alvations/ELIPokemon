# The general became public and the particular did not, and both common errors treat that as one change

The old asymmetry had two components, and they were held by the same person. The clinician held
the **general** — which conditions exist, what they do, what the evidence says, what the words
mean — and also the **particular**: this person's history, the examination, the record over time,
and the ability to order and interpret a test. Only one of those two has become public.

The general is now, for practical purposes, fully available. Anyone can read a description of any
condition, the published evidence on its treatment, and the experience of thousands of people who
have it. The particular has not moved at all: it still requires an examination, a record,
continuity, and access to investigation. So the reversal is real, it is substantial, and it is
**one-dimensional** — which is why an argument about who knows more is the wrong argument, and why
both of the common errors in this consultation are versions of the same mistake (**mechanism**).

Load-bearing claims below are marked with what they rest on: **mechanism**, **definitional**,
(**consensus**) or (**country-dependent**).

## What is found is not wrong, but it has a predictable shape

The useful framing is not accuracy. The quality of what is available ranges widely, the
best-known reference sources are written to be reliable, and in any case accuracy is not where the
clinically interesting failure sits. What is found has four structural properties that hold
*independently* of whether it is accurate, and all four matter clinically (**mechanism**).

1. **It is not ordered by probability.** Search results and reference pages are sorted by
   searchability, recency, severity and engagement — not by prevalence in the reader's population.
   So a list of possible causes arrives *unranked*, and the serious rare ones are over-represented
   relative to their frequency. m011 is the argument about what prevalence does to the meaning of
   a finding; the person's reading is missing exactly that term.
2. **It does not say which population it came from.** A statement about how often a symptom
   indicates something serious is a number fitted in whatever population the study recruited,
   usually a referred or hospital one. m111 is the full argument about what fails to transport,
   and the thing that fails worst is the prior. Nothing in the text usually states the setting, so
   a correct statement arrives detached from the condition that made it correct.
3. **Absence of a mention reads as absence of the thing.** If a page does not mention a
   possibility, that is indistinguishable from the possibility having been considered and
   excluded. This is nursing m073's collapse — a storage class that fails quietly into "no" — in a
   different medium.
4. **It answers the question asked.** A search is a query, and the result is conditional on the
   query. A person who begins from a fear reads material that matches it, which is not a criticism
   of them: it is a property of retrieval, and it is the same property that makes the opening
   question of a consultation shape the diagnosis in m048.

What the clinician holds that none of that supplies is the **prior** — how common this is, in a
person like this, presenting this way, here — and the particular. Those are the two missing terms.
The consultation goes well when it supplies them and badly when it tries to contest the general
instead, because the general is usually not where the error is (**mechanism**).

## Why dismissal is a diagnostic error and not only a discourtesy

Three separate mechanisms, and the first is the one that gets underweighted.

**What someone searched for is a history item.** It tells you what they are afraid of, which is
clinical information: m114's argument is that expressed concern carries information, and that the
asymmetry runs in one direction — high concern carries more than low. A person who has read about
one condition has told you something about their presentation before saying anything else.
Discarding it discards data.

**It identifies the question that has to be answered.** An unanswered question does not go away;
it generates another consultation, another search, or a loss of confidence in the answer that was
given. m138's re-consultation loop is the mechanism, and it consumes exactly the capacity that the
brisker consultation saved.

**Sometimes the person is right, and the circumstances in which they are right are predictable.**
A generalist holds breadth; a person with one condition can hold more depth in it than any
generalist will. For a rare diagnosis — especially one with a long pre-diagnostic history and a
characteristic cluster that is unfamiliar — the person who has read everything about it is a
genuinely informative source, and the published accounts of long diagnostic delays contain a great
many instances of somebody having named the answer early (**consensus**). The clinical skill is
not to decide in advance which situation this is; it is to hold both open.

And the opposite error is just as real. Over-accommodation — ordering a test to settle a question
the test cannot settle — buys a short consultation and a long cascade: a result that needs
explaining, an incidental finding, a referral, and a label. m084 holds that argument in full, and
the relevant part here is that the harm arrives much later and attaches to nobody.

## What the consultation actually does with it

* **Ask what they found and what worries them most.** Two questions, and the second one does most
  of the work. It is the fastest available route to the thing the consultation has to resolve.
* **Supply the missing terms rather than contesting the general.** The prior, the examination, and
  what the record shows over time. "That is a real condition and this is why it is unlikely to be
  what is happening here" is a different statement from "that is not a thing", and only one of
  them is usually true.
* **Name the class of the decision.** m083 separates effectiveness-sensitive from
  preference-sensitive, and a great deal of what arrives pre-researched is in the second class —
  where there is no single right answer and the person's own weighting is a required input rather
  than an interference.
* **Be explicit about what is not known.** Uncertainty stated plainly is more durable than
  confidence that gets contradicted by the next page they read.
* **Treat a tracked series as data with measurement properties.** When somebody brings home
  measurements or device output, the clinically interesting questions are when they measured, why
  they measured then, what the device is, and what it actually reports — not whether
  patient-generated data is admissible. Self-selected measurement times are a sampling problem,
  and nursing m109 holds the general argument about what sampling buys and costs.
* **Record what was discussed and why.** The next clinician inherits a decision that looks
  arbitrary without it, and an unexplained decision gets quietly reversed.

## Two things this changes about the system, not just the room

**It is unequally distributed, so responding to it well can widen a gap.** The people who arrive
with printed pages, a reading list and a tracked series are not a random sample of the list: they
skew towards people with time, confidence, language, connectivity and previous experience of being
taken seriously. A service that is more responsive to a prepared patient is, without anybody
deciding this, less responsive to everybody else. m050's inverse care law is the mechanism, and
the counterweight has to be deliberate: the question "what are you most worried about" costs the
same for everybody and is the one intervention that does not require the person to have prepared.

**Automated symptom checkers and generated answers are a different object from a reference page**,
because they produce something that looks like a particular answer from general information plus
what the person typed. They have no examination and no prior, they are fluent, and the output's
confidence is not related to its accuracy. The structural point stands unchanged: what they cannot
have is the right-hand column. How well any specific tool performs is an empirical question, it
differs by tool and by presentation, and the evidence is arriving rather than settled
(**consensus** on the structural limit; performance contested).

## The human stakes, said plainly

People look things up because they are frightened and because waiting is hard. That is the whole
of the motivation and it does not need any other explanation. Reading at three in the morning
about something that might be happening to you is not a failure of discipline; it is what anybody
does.

The experience of being dismissed for having read about it is corrosive out of all proportion to
the consultation it happens in. It is reported most by people whose symptoms are hard to see,
whose conditions fluctuate, who are young, who are women, and who have been doubted before — and
the effect is not only that one question goes unanswered. It changes whether they come back, what
they say when they do, and whether they believe the reassurance when it is finally correct. Being
disbelieved while unwell is its own injury, and it is distributed unequally.

And the honest half from the other side. A consultation that arrives with a conclusion already
formed is harder work, it takes longer than the time available, and the clinician is sometimes
being asked to disprove something that cannot be disproved — which is not a reasonable request and
cannot be met by agreeing to it. Saying plainly that a test cannot answer the question, and
staying in the room afterwards, is a genuinely difficult thing to do under time pressure. Naming
that difficulty is more useful than pretending the skilled version of this consultation is easy.

## What an examiner digs into next

Whether the candidate separates general from particular knowledge without being prompted, and
states that only one of them moved. Then the bottom-right cell: that self-tracking adds particular
data from the person's side, with its own sampling problem. Then the four structural properties of
what is found, with the unranked differential and the missing population identified as the two
that matter. Then why dismissal is a diagnostic error, with the search treated as a history item.
Then the conditions under which the person is more likely to be right, stated as a structural
consequence of breadth against depth. Then the opposite error, with the cascade attached. Then the
equity consequence and the one counterweight that does not require preparation. Then the hard one
— what they would say to somebody asking for a test that cannot answer their question, and what
they would do in the same consultation afterwards.

## Scope and safety

This is revision material about the structure of a consultation, written for someone already
training in or qualified for the field. It is not a clinical reference and not a decision aid.
**It is not advice about how any individual should use health information about themselves**, and
nothing here says whether any particular source, website, application or symptom checker is
reliable — that is a question about specific tools, it changes, and it is not answerable from
here. No test, threshold, condition or probability is named in this answer on purpose. If you are
a patient reading this: looking things up is reasonable, and the one thing worth bringing to a
consultation is what you are most worried about. If someone is unwell right now, the relevant
action is to contact local urgent care or the local emergency number, not to read this.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The structural claim — that the general became public while the particular did not, so the
reversal is one-dimensional — is mechanism and will not move. So are the four properties of what
is found, the treatment of a search as a history item, and the unequal distribution. What is
moving quickly is the technology: generated answers, consumer devices producing continuous
physiological data, direct-to-consumer testing, and patient access to their own records are all
changing what arrives in the room, and each of them changes the *right-hand* column as well as the
left. That is the development most likely to date this answer, because it attacks the one half of
the asymmetry that has so far held. Take any claim about a specific tool's performance from a
current evaluation rather than from here, as of October 2026.
