# The mechanism is a prior over injuries, and the same finding means different things under it

The mechanism of injury is not a finding. It is a description of a physical event — how much
energy was transferred, in what direction, over what period, into which tissues, with the body in
what state at the moment of transfer — and what it buys is a **prior probability distribution over
possible injuries**, before anybody has looked at anything. The examination is then the same
examination. What changes is the weight each of its results carries. **(Mechanism** — this is
Bayesian reasoning applied to a physical event, and it is checkable by reasoning rather than by
citation.**)**

Stated that way the apparent paradox dissolves. A given finding — a tender area, a normal
examination, an equivocal result — has a different posterior under a different prior, so acting
differently on the same finding is not inconsistency. It is the only coherent response to having
different information.

## What the energy did, and what declares late

```
   the event                        what it determines
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   how much energy                  the magnitude of the possible injury
   over what distance and time       whether it was absorbed or transmitted
   in what direction                 the line of force, and so the SET of injuries
   into which tissue first           what is in the path behind the entry point
   state of the body at the time     restrained or not; braced or not; relaxed or not;
                                     which way the head and neck were turned
   properties of that body           bone quality, age, prior surgery, pregnancy,
                                     anticoagulation, the tissues' own tolerance
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                     │
                     ▼
   a ranked list of injuries to look for, including the ones that
   ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  are fully determined at the moment of transfer               │
   │  and produce NO sign for minutes to hours afterwards          │
   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                     │
                     ▼
         these are the ones the prior exists to catch, because
         waiting for them to declare themselves is the failure mode
```

Three things the prior does, and they are different from each other.

**It raises the probability of specific injuries that are occult early.** Some consequences of
energy transfer are complete at the instant it happens and silent for a long time afterwards. No
examination at the time can find them, because there is nothing yet to find. The only way they are
caught early is that something about the event said to look for them, so they are actively sought,
imaged, or observed over time rather than waited for. This is the single highest-value thing the
mechanism contributes.

**It sets the search pattern rather than the search target.** Energy travels in a line, and a
second injury along that same line is more likely than chance. So the mechanism tells you not just
what to look for but *where else* — the associated injury, the one on the far side, the one above
and below. An examination guided by a line of force covers different ground from one guided by
where the patient says it hurts. **(Consensus** that injuries associate along a line of force and
that structured trauma assessment is built around that; **country-dependent** in which
associations each trauma network chooses to name in its own guidance.**)**

**It changes the threshold for acting on an equivocal result.** Where the prior is high, a
borderline finding is treated as positive; where it is low, the same finding is reasonable to
observe. This is the mechanism's effect on decisions rather than on observations, and it is where
it does most of its day-to-day work.

## Why it is the mechanics and not the label

The label of an event is a poor summary of the physics, and this is where reasoning about
mechanism earns its keep over memorising categories.

The same nominal event — a fall, a collision, a blow — spans an enormous range of energy depending
on height, speed, the surface, what intervened, and whether the body was restrained or braced. The
same energy into the same place produces different injuries in bone of different quality, in a
body of different size, and in a person whose clotting is impaired. And identical energy in a
different direction, or with the head turned, loads entirely different structures. So the useful
question is never *what kind of event was it* but *what was transferred, where, into what, in what
state*. A classification that stops at the label will produce the same prior for events whose
physics differ by an order of magnitude. **(Mechanism.)**

## Four ways the prior misleads, each with a name

* **Anchoring.** The prior is formed first and is the hardest thing to revise. A severe mechanism
  frames everything found afterwards, and findings that do not fit get explained away. The
  reasoning that correctly raises suspicion is the same reasoning that correctly resists being
  revised, and those are the same cognitive machinery.
* **Reassurance from a benign mechanism.** This is the dangerous direction. A low-energy account
  talking a clinician out of a real, present, abnormal finding is the prior overriding the
  observation, which is exactly backwards: the observation is data about this patient, and the
  prior is data about events like this one. In the very old, in the very young and in anyone whose
  tissues tolerate less, a mechanism that sounds trivial is routinely not.
* **The account is often wrong, incomplete or absent.** Nobody saw it; the patient does not
  remember; the person describing it was not there; what is reported is what was inferred
  afterwards. A prior built on a reported mechanism inherits the reliability of the report, and
  that uncertainty has to be carried forward rather than discarded once the history is written
  down.
* **An unpredicted finding does not discredit the mechanism, and a predicted one that is absent
  does not either.** The prior is a distribution over events of this kind, not a claim about this
  one. A finding the mechanism did not suggest is still a finding and is acted on; an injury the
  mechanism strongly suggested and that is genuinely absent is simply the common case for any
  prediction well short of certainty. Both directions get misread as evidence that the reasoning
  failed, and neither is.
* **A high-risk mechanism cannot be used in reverse.** The absence of a concerning mechanism does
  not exclude an injury, and a reassuring mechanism is not a negative test. Mechanism-based
  criteria are built to be sensitive at the cost of specificity, which means they over-trigger by
  design and cannot be read as rule-outs. **(Consensus** that this is how such criteria are
  constructed; the criteria themselves are **country-dependent**, differ between trauma networks,
  are revised, and none is stated here.**)**

## Why the prior has to be written down and handed on

Everything above lives in the head of whoever was told about the event, and almost none of it is
recoverable later. The scene is gone. The vehicle has been moved. The height is an estimate nobody
will be able to re-make. So the mechanism is one of the items whose loss at a handover is least
recoverable and most costly, which is question m070's subject; and the practical consequence is
that the mechanism belongs in the record as specifics — energy, direction, state of the body — and
not as a label, because the label is the part that survives and the specifics are the part that
carries the information.

## The human stakes, said plainly

What the prior is for is a person in whom something has already happened and has not yet announced
itself.

The injury that is complete at the moment of transfer and silent for hours afterwards is the
reason this reasoning exists, and missing one is not a near-miss: it is a person who was examined,
found normal, and was deteriorating the whole time. Delayed recognition of injury is a
well-described and much-studied failure, and the structure of this answer is the structure of why
it happens — the examination was correct and the prior was never set, so nobody was looking.
**(Consensus** that occult and delayed-presenting injury is a major source of avoidable harm in
trauma care and that mechanism-based criteria exist for that reason.**)**

Three things belong here that are about people rather than physics. The person who was there is
frequently frightened, frequently in pain, and sometimes does not remember the event at all, so
the history may have to come from someone else, and that someone else may be distressed too — the
quality of the account is a function of the circumstances in which it was given. A mechanism that
sounds minor in a person whose tissues tolerate less deserves more caution rather than less, and
the people most often under-triaged for exactly this reason are the old. And nothing about
reasoning from mechanism is an account of what anybody did or should have done: it is a statement
about what energy does to tissue, not about blame, and reading it as the latter is both wrong and
unhelpful.

This answer stays at the level of why the reasoning is structured as it is. It states no criteria,
no imaging indications, no thresholds and no sequence of actions, because those differ by trauma
network and by institution, they are revised, and acting on a remembered version of someone else's
is the harm this material exists to avoid.

## Scope and safety

**If someone is unwell right now, call your local emergency number.** This is not for use during
an emergency, and reading this instead of calling for help would be worse than doing nothing at
all.

This is revision material about *why a kind of reasoning is structured the way it is*, written for
someone already trained. It is deliberately not a protocol and not a decision aid: it contains no
triage criteria, no imaging indications, no thresholds, no sequence of actions, no doses and no
settings, and it is not something to consult while acting. Mechanism-based criteria differ
substantially between trauma networks and between countries and are revised on a cycle. The
reader's own network guidance and local policy are the authority; this is not, and it has had no
clinical review. Nothing here describes any real person, any real case or any real event.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The reasoning is stable and is not controversial: that the mechanism of injury functions as a
prior rather than as a finding, that it earns its place mainly by pointing at injuries which are
silent early, and that it cannot be used in reverse as a rule-out are long-standing and follow
from biomechanics and from probability rather than from any single document. What is not stable is
every specific built on top of it. The mechanism-based criteria that trigger a trauma call, the
imaging indications attached to particular events, the age and comorbidity modifiers, and the
vocabulary each network uses all differ between trauma systems and have been revised repeatedly —
and under-triage of older patients in mechanism-based criteria has been an active area of revision
for years. Treat this as an explanation of a principle dated October 2026, and read the current
document from the trauma network and institution that govern where the reader actually works for
anything beyond the principle.
