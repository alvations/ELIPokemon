# The label names where the cause is, and is routinely read as naming how much it matters

Somebody arrives and the presenting problem is not a symptom. It is that the arrangement they were
living under has stopped working: they cannot get up the stairs any more, the person who was
helping cannot help this week, they have not eaten, they were found on the floor. The word usually
attached to this is *social*, and the word is doing two jobs at once. As a statement about
**where the cause is**, it can be accurate. As a statement about **how much the problem matters,
how urgent it is, or whether a disease is present**, it is not a statement at all — and it is read
as one constantly. **(Definitional** — this is about what the label denotes, not a clinical
claim.**)**

Everything else in this answer follows from separating those two readings.

## Function is an output of two terms, and it fails where the margin is smallest

A living arrangement works when what a person can do exceeds what they are being asked to do, with
some margin. That is two quantities, not one, and the margin is the thing that determines whether
anything is noticed.

```
   capability                               demand
   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   strength, balance, stamina         vs    stairs, distance to the bathroom, the weight
   vision, hearing, cognition               of a kettle, heating that needs lighting
   continence, swallowing                   the hours nobody is there
   mood, motivation, pain                   what has to be remembered and when
   the medicines and their effects          who else depends on them
   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                        margin = capability − demand
                                   │
      a small decrement ANYWHERE   │   a small increase in demand ANYWHERE
      — a new infection, a drug    ▼   — a carer's own illness, a broken boiler,
        effect, pain, a night   margin   a hospital stay that changed nothing
        without sleep          < 0       at home, a helper who is no longer there
                                   │
                                   ▼
            the presenting feature is the ARRANGEMENT failing.
            It is not the decrement, and it does not name the decrement.
```

Read that way, an arrival of this kind is a signal that **something changed**, and the clinical
question is which term moved and why. A person whose margin was always small needs only a tiny
decrement to cross zero, which means the size of the presenting event carries almost no
information about the size of the cause. **(Mechanism** — this follows from the structure of a
threshold system.**)**

That is the same reasoning as question m034's compensation: a system holding an output steady is
closer to failing than its output suggests, and the margin is not displayed on anything.

## Much of the modifiable risk is in the second term

The decrement is often the medical half and the demand is often where the available change is. A
stair that cannot be climbed can be answered by treating the hip, and it can be answered by the
bathroom being downstairs; which is achievable depends on circumstances that are not clinical at
all.

Two consequences, both mechanical.

* **Treating the first term and returning someone to an unchanged second term reproduces the
  presentation.** That is not non-adherence and it is not a person *failing to cope*; it is an
  arithmetic identity. If the demand side is untouched, the margin after treatment is the margin
  before it plus whatever the treatment bought. **(Mechanism.)**
* **The hazard frequently belongs to the environment and was set by a process that has since
  left.** A deconditioning admission months ago, a medicine started elsewhere, a service
  withdrawn, a helper who is no longer there. Nobody currently involved set it, everybody
  currently involved is dealing with it, and nothing in the person's own body is the thing to be
  modified. This is question m036's device exactly — the hazard is on the ground, not on the
  patient, and whoever laid it has gone.

## Why dismissal is a diagnostic error, and not merely unkind

The error has a specific shape. A label that names the **location** of a cause is used as if it
named the **absence** of one, and the label then suppresses the search that would test it.

Three facts give that error teeth.

* **Acute illness in older people frequently presents as a change in function rather than as the
  classic features of the disease** — a fall, immobility, not eating, new confusion, or simply
  being unable to manage what was manageable last week. The presentation and the mechanism are
  separated by a step that has to be taken deliberately. **(Consensus.)**
* **The assessment that finds the decrement is often not the one that was done.** A label applied
  before a history of the change — what was the person able to do a week ago, and what happened
  since — is applied before the evidence that would refute it. **(Consensus** that a proportion of
  presentations recorded this way have an identifiable acute problem on fuller assessment; the
  proportion is a matter of published series and is not quantified here.**)**
* **"No medical cause found" is unfalsifiable unless somebody wrote down what was looked for.**
  Absence of a recorded search is not a negative finding, which is question m070's missing
  pertinent negative and question m037's distinction between what was seen and what was
  confirmed.

The signature in a record is recognisable: the label appears early, it appears before any account
of the change, and every later entry is consistent with it because every later reader inherited
it. Question m068 describes the same mechanism under the name anchoring; question m048 describes
it as premature closure. The only unusual feature here is that the anchor arrives as a category
rather than as a diagnosis, which makes it harder to notice and harder to argue with.

## What the structured response is, and why it is not one person's job

The structured answer to this presentation is a multidisciplinary assessment of both terms:
function now against function before, the medicines, mobility, nutrition, continence, cognition,
mood, and the actual physical and human situation the person is returning to. It is
multidisciplinary because the two terms are assessed by different professions and because the
second term cannot be changed by a prescription. **(Consensus** that comprehensive assessment of
this kind is the recognised response and that it is multidisciplinary; its name, its composition,
what it is funded to do and where it happens are **country-dependent** and differ sharply between
systems.**)**

Deliberately outside this answer: anything about capacity, consent, substitute decision-making or
protection of adults at risk. Those sit inside specific legal frameworks that differ by
jurisdiction, they are not reasoning anyone should do from a revision note, and a general account
of them would be worse than none.

## The human stakes, said plainly

The word is said out loud in front of people, and they hear it. A person who has arrived because
their life stopped working, and who is then described as *a social*, has been told that they are
an administrative problem rather than a patient — and they are usually already afraid that this is
true, because the thing that has happened to them is the loss of the arrangement that made
independence possible.

Three things follow. The first is that the vocabulary is not cosmetic: a presentation described as
a failure of the person invites a different sequence of actions from one described as a change to
be explained, and the two sequences find different things. The second is that the people best
placed to describe the decrement — the person themselves, whoever was helping them, whoever saw
them last week — are frequently the people nobody asked, and their account is primary data rather
than background. The third is that carers are often the reason the arrangement worked at all, and
a carer's own illness or exhaustion is a clinical fact about this presentation even though it is a
fact about somebody who is not the patient.

And the honest closing point is that the frustration in a department under pressure is real and is
not a reason. The problem is genuinely one the department cannot solve on its own, the resources
to change the second term are usually somewhere else, and the correct response to a problem whose
solution lies elsewhere is to characterise it accurately and refer it, which is not the same as
declining to assess it. Where the structural problem gets attached to the person in front of you,
that is the failure.

Nothing above is a protocol. No assessment tool, no score, no threshold and no pathway appears,
because each is local, is revised, and belongs to the reader's own guidance.

## What an examiner digs into next

* The word *social* is doing two jobs. Name them, and say which one is the error.
* Write function as two terms. Why does the size of the presenting event say so little about the
  size of the cause?
* Why does treating the medical decrement and discharging to an unchanged situation reproduce the
  presentation?
* How does acute illness in an older person present differently, and what step does that force?
* Why is "no medical cause found" unfalsifiable without a recorded search?
* What is the signature of this error in a written record?
* Why is the structured response multidisciplinary rather than medical?

## Scope and safety

**If someone is unwell right now, call your local emergency number.** This is not for use during

all.

This is revision material about *how a presentation is categorised and why the categorisation
misleads*, written for someone already trained. It is deliberately not a protocol: it names no
assessment tool, no score, no threshold and no pathway, and it is not something to consult while
assessing anyone. It contains no account of capacity, consent, substitute decision-making or the

## Where this stands, October 2026

The argument is stable: that the label names a location rather than an absence, that function is a
margin between two terms, that a small decrement crosses a small margin, that much of the
modifiable risk sits in the demand term, that acute illness in older people presents as functional
change, and that an unrecorded search is not a negative finding are all long-standing teaching and
follow from the structure of the problem rather than from any single document. What is not stable
is everything about delivery. What comprehensive assessment is called, who is in the team, whether
it exists at the front door at all, what community services can be arranged and how quickly, and
the standards against which any of it is measured differ sharply between countries and between
institutions and move with each reorganisation. The law on capacity and on the protection of
adults at risk differs by jurisdiction and is not described here at all. Treat this as an
explanation of a principle dated October 2026, and read the current guidance from the society,
college and institution that govern where the reader actually works for anything beyond the
principle.
