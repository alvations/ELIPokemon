# Two routes reach one bloodstream, the dressing covers one of them, and the count erases both.

m038 is the argument that every indwelling device is a standing trade against infection risk and
that duration is the dominant term; it hands this answer the duration term and the daily review of
necessity, and this answer takes up what happens while the device is still justified. The whole
subject rests on one fact that is easy to state and routinely lost: **organisms reach the
bloodstream along the outside of the catheter or along the inside of it, these are different
mechanisms with different interventions, and the number that gets reported cannot tell them
apart.**

Markers used below: (**mechanism**), (**definitional**), (**consensus**),
(**country-dependent**). No dwell time, dressing change interval, antiseptic concentration,
scrub duration or infection rate appears anywhere here. All of those are set by the reader's
national guidance and local policy.

*The route distinction is mechanistic; the shift in predominance with dwell time is consensus

Notice the consequence for troubleshooting, because it is where the distinction earns its place:
an institution with a rising infection rate and immaculate insertion practice is probably looking
at the wrong route (**mechanism**). The insertion bundle cannot fix an intraluminal problem, and
auditing it harder will produce excellent compliance figures and no change.

that moves is a device being inoculated; and occluded moisture is more dangerous than open
moisture because the occlusion is what turns it into a culture medium (**mechanism**). The
transparent semipermeable choice is consensus across major national guidance rather than
mechanism, because what it optimises is the cost of looking (**consensus**).

Two practical points follow. **Integrity, not the calendar, is what makes a dressing need
changing** — a scheduled change is a backstop for a dressing that has stayed intact, and the
schedule is local (**country-dependent**). And **the skin under the dressing is the thing being
inspected**, not the dressing: redness, exudate, tenderness and discolouration at the site are the
findings, and the whole reason the dressing is transparent is to make looking cheap.

That asymmetry is mechanism rather than guidance: an act performed once by identifiable people
under observation and an act repeated indefinitely by everyone on every shift have different
failure rates for structural reasons, whatever either bundle contains (**mechanism**).

And there is a second structural feature of the maintenance half worth stating, because it
explains most residual risk. Protection against the intraluminal route is not a barrier at all.
It is **a list of specified acts** — decontaminate the hub before access, change the set at this
point, use this technique for this task — and an access event that nobody wrote a rule for is
simply uncovered (**mechanism**). The extraluminal route has a physical barrier that works
whether or not anyone is thinking about it; the intraluminal route has only the list. So the
useful question about a local policy is not whether its elements are right but whether the list
covers every way the lumen is actually opened on this ward: blood sampling, flushing, a new
infusion, a connector change, a transducer, an emergency access in the middle of the night.

*The elements named are consensus across major national guidance. The specific antiseptic
preparation, its concentration, the dressing type, the set change intervals and the dwell
limits are all (**country-dependent**) and deliberately not stated here.*

## Why all-or-none, and what the measure is for

A bundle is a small set of evidence-based elements applied together, measured as **all-or-none
compliance**: an episode counts only if every element was performed (**definitional**).

The arithmetic is the whole argument. If each element of a five-element bundle is performed
independently and reliably most of the time, the probability that *all five* were performed in a
given episode is the product, not the average — so a set of individually respectable element
figures produces an all-or-none figure that is substantially lower, and the gap grows with the
number of elements (**mechanism**). Reporting element by element therefore hides exactly the
thing the bundle exists to deliver, because the pathway needs every link and the patient
experiences the product.

Two further things the all-or-none measure is doing, which are often missed:

* It makes the **absence of an element visible as a defect** rather than as a difference of
  opinion. The point of a bundle is not that each element is individually powerful — some are
  weakly evidenced on their own — but that the set is applied consistently and that deviation
  becomes something to explain (**consensus**).
* It is a **measure of a system, not a grade for a person.** A bundle used punitively produces
  documentation compliance rather than practice change, and documentation compliance is the one
  failure mode the measure cannot detect from its own data (**mechanism**).

Both of those are definitions rather than findings, and the fact that two definitions of one event
produce two different numbers follows from what each was built for (**definitional**); that a rate
moves when the definition, the ascertainment or the culturing practice moves is mechanism
(**mechanism**).

*Both definitions, their criteria and the national surveillance programme that uses them are
(**country-dependent**) and are revised; the reader's own programme is the authority on which
version is in force.*

The practical consequence is that a falling rate is evidence of something and not proof of
anything, and that a rate compared between two institutions is comparing two ascertainment
systems as much as two sets of practice.

## The failure modes

**The dressing changed on the calendar and not on its integrity**, in both directions: a sound
dressing disturbed for no reason, and a lifted one left because it is not due.

**The hub.** The single most common gap between a good insertion and a good outcome, because it is
the step that recurs, that has no record, and that is performed under time pressure by whoever is
giving the next medicine.

**The line that is still there.** m038's argument, and it belongs here as the first question of
every round rather than as a periodic audit.

**The blood culture drawn through the device.** It contaminates easily, it answers a question
about the lumen rather than about the patient, and the interpretation differs from a peripheral
sample. What is required locally — paired cultures, which site first, whether drawing through the
line is permitted at all — is protocol, and m132 is the argument about why the collection and not
the laboratory decides what can be reported.

**The bundle audited as a form.** A checklist completed after the event measures the form.

**Treating all vascular access as one category.** A short peripheral cannula, a midline, a
peripherally inserted central catheter, a tunnelled line and an implanted port differ in insertion
risk, dwell, what may be infused through them and how they are accessed. The *structure* of the
argument is identical across all of them, which is why m038 can treat them together; the numbers
and the procedures are not, which is why the policy cannot.

## The human stakes, said plainly

A line infection is a serious event. It is not a complication in the sense of an inconvenience: it
means bacteraemia in someone who is usually already unwell, often the removal and replacement of
access that was difficult to obtain in the first place, a prolonged course of treatment, and a
longer stay. It is also, substantially, preventable, and that combination is what makes this
subject carry the weight it does.

The person at the other end of it is frequently someone whose life depends on that access
continuing to work: someone on long-term parenteral nutrition, someone having chemotherapy,
someone on dialysis, a child who has had many lines and is running out of sites. For them a
central line is not a clinical object but a piece of their body that they live with, protect, and
know a great deal about — often more than the person accessing it on a given shift. The single
most useful practical habit in the whole subject is to ask them how their line is usually handled,
and the single most common insult is to override the answer.

Two things about how this gets discussed. Zero-tolerance framing has driven real improvement and
has a cost: where a rate is a performance measure, the pressure is on ascertainment as well as on
practice, and the person who orders the blood culture that finds an infection is the person who
creates the number. And when an infection does occur, treating it as a personal failure in a
distributed, interruption-driven process is both unjust and ineffective — the maintenance bundle
is distributed across everyone on every shift, which is precisely why individual blame cannot
locate the defect.

And the context: hub decontamination before every access, aseptic technique and the time to do a
dressing properly are all time-dependent tasks performed under interruption. An institution that
responds to its line infection rate with a teaching session, having changed neither staffing nor
the number of access events a patient receives, has identified the wrong variable.

## Scope and safety

This explains what a vascular access dressing protects and why bundles are measured as
all-or-none, for someone already training in or qualified for clinical practice. It is not a
vascular access policy, not an insertion or dressing procedure and not a guide to diagnosing or
managing any infection. It deliberately states no dwell time, dressing change interval, antiseptic
agent or concentration, scrub duration, administration set interval or infection rate, because
every one of those is set by the reader's national guidance and institutional policy, which are
the authority. It is not a basis for any decision about whether a device should be removed, nor
for starting, changing or withholding any treatment. Nothing here has had clinical or
microbiological review. Nothing here is for use in an emergency or for a decision about any
person's care. If someone is unwell right now, the local emergency number is the correct response.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The route distinction and the all-or-none arithmetic are structural and do not date. What moves,
and moves often, is everything procedural: antiseptic preparations, dressing types including
antimicrobial-impregnated ones, needleless connector design and disinfection methods,
administration set intervals and the evidence for locking solutions are all active areas and the
recommendations differ by country at any given moment. The surveillance definitions move too, and
a rate reported under a revised definition is not comparable with the one before it — which is
why this answer names neither. The one direction of travel that looks durable is the shift of
attention from insertion, where practice is now generally good, toward maintenance and toward
reducing the number of access events a device receives, which is the term the structure of this
answer predicts should matter most. The reader's national guidance, institutional policy and
surveillance programme are the authority throughout.
