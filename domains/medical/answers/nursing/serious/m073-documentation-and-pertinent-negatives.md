# The record is not a description of care that happened. It is the input to the next decision.

Everything that is hard about documentation follows from one reframe. The record is usually taught
as an account rendered afterwards — proof of work, protection in a complaint, material for an
audit. Those are real, and they are secondary. Its primary function is that **somebody who was not
there will read it and act on it**, often at three in the morning, often without being able to ask
you anything. *This is mechanism rather than guideline; the specific duties below are
country-dependent.*

Markers used below: *mechanism*, *definitional*, *consensus*, *country-dependent*.

That reframe decides every contested question. Is this detail worth recording? Would the next
person act differently if they had it. Is this abbreviation acceptable? Will the next person read
it the same way. Should I write "chest clear"? That is the pertinent-negative question, and it has
a structural answer rather than a stylistic one.

## The three states, and the one the record cannot distinguish

```
   what was true                        what the record says       what the next reader can infer
   ──────────────────────────────────── ───────────────────────── ──────────────────────────────
   asked / looked, and found something   "productive cough"        a positive finding. Act.
   ──────────────────────────────────── ───────────────────────── ──────────────────────────────
   asked / looked, and found nothing     "no cough"                a NEGATIVE finding, which is
                                                                   information: the question has
                                                                   been answered, by a named
                                                                   person, at a known time
   ──────────────────────────────────── ───────────────────────── ──────────────────────────────
   did not ask, did not look             nothing                   nothing
   ──────────────────────────────────── ───────────────────────── ──────────────────────────────
   asked, found nothing, did not write   nothing                   nothing
   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   FOUR real states. THREE possible entries. The last two states produce an IDENTICAL
   blank, and no amount of reading the chart separates them. That collapse is the whole
   reason a pertinent negative is written down: it is the only way to turn "I checked and
   it was fine" into something that survives the end of your shift.
```

A **pertinent negative** is the recorded absence of a finding that would have changed the
interpretation if it had been present. *Definitional.* It is not a licence to document every
absence — the word carrying the weight is *pertinent*, and it is pertinent in relation to the
differential being entertained and the risk being excluded. "No new confusion" is pertinent in
someone being watched for delirium and is noise in someone else's record. The judgement about
which negatives are pertinent is clinical, and it is the part that cannot be systematised.

## Why "not documented" is read as "not done"

In review, audit and legal process, an undocumented action is generally treated as an action that
did not happen. *Consensus across jurisdictions in substance; the precise doctrine, the standard
of proof and the professional-regulatory consequences differ by country, and the reader's own
regulator and legal framework are the authority.*

Stated as a complaint this sounds unjust, and sometimes it is — things are done and not written.
But the reason the default exists is the collapse in the table above: **the reviewer cannot
distinguish "not done" from "done and unrecorded" either.** Faced with two indistinguishable
states, a process has to pick a default, and the only safe one for future patients is the
pessimistic one. Arguing with the default is less useful than understanding that it is forced.

## What a usable entry contains

Not a template — a function, from the "somebody will act on this" premise:

```
   element                            why the next reader needs it
   ─────────────────────────────────  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   WHEN, and when it was written      a retrospective entry is a different object from a
                                      contemporaneous one, and must be visibly marked as one
   WHO, identifiably                  so the next reader can ask, and so accountability is
                                      locatable. Role as well as name
   WHAT WAS OBSERVED                  the observation, not only the score derived from it.
                                      m001 is the full argument: the composite is a summary
                                      and the summary is not the data
   THE PERTINENT NEGATIVES            what was looked for and not found
   WHAT WAS DONE                      including what was not done, and why not
   THE REASONING, briefly             the next person inherits a decision; without the
                                      "because", they cannot tell what would change it
   THE PLAN AND ITS TRIGGER           what happens next, and the specific finding that
                                      should prompt escalation. m002's thresholds live here
   WHO WAS TOLD                       escalation that is not recorded is indistinguishable
                                      from escalation that did not happen — and this is the
                                      single most consequential omission in the list
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

*The elements are consensus across professional record-keeping standards. The format they are
written in — structured note, flowsheet, free text, a named framework — is institutional.*

## The failure modes, which are mostly structural

**Copy-forward.** An electronic record makes it trivial to carry yesterday's examination into
today. The entry then looks contemporaneous, is attributed to today's clinician, and may describe
a state that no longer exists. *Consensus; one of the most consistently reported electronic record
defects.*

**The mandatory field.** A field that must be completed before the form will submit gets
completed. What it does not get is thought, and a default value that was never considered is worse
than a blank, because it reads as a finding. *Mechanism.*

**Recording the score instead of the observation.** Common, convenient, and it destroys exactly
the information that made the composite useful. See m001.

**Writing the plan and omitting the finding.** "Continue antibiotics" records a decision whose
basis is now unrecoverable.

**Abbreviation and local shorthand.** Fine within a team, dangerous across a boundary, and some
abbreviations have more than one accepted meaning. *Consensus; which abbreviations are prohibited
is country- and institution-dependent.*

**Correction by obliteration.** Correcting an error is normal and expected; destroying what was
there is not, because the sequence of what was believed and when is itself clinical information.
*Consensus in principle; the mechanics — strike through, date, sign, or the electronic audit trail
— are set by local policy.*

**Volume as a substitute for signal.** A long entry that buries the one finding that matters has
failed the reader as completely as a short one that omits it.

## The human stakes, said plainly

Documentation is the part of the job most often done tired, last, and after the thing that needed
doing. It is also the part that reaches furthest: an entry written in four minutes is read by
people who will never meet you, for years, and sometimes in circumstances nobody anticipated.

Two consequences follow for real people. The first is that records are read by patients and
families, increasingly as a right of access, and the words in them land. A phrase like "poor
historian" or "refused care" carries a judgement that travels with someone through every
subsequent encounter, and the discipline of writing what was observed rather than what was
concluded about the person is a kindness as well as a professional standard.

The second is that the record is often the only trace a patient leaves of having tried to raise
something. "Daughter concerned that he is not himself" is an entry that has changed outcomes. The
absence of that entry is not neutral; it is the removal of the one piece of evidence that someone
noticed early.

And the context has to be said: documentation quality is substantially a function of time,
staffing and the usability of the system. An institution that responds to a records failure by
asking individuals to document better, without changing any of those three, has identified the
wrong variable.

## What an examiner digs into next

What a pertinent negative is, and which negatives are pertinent in a specific scenario. Why the
reviewer's default of "not documented means not done" is forced rather than unfair. Which of the
four states in the table collapse, and why that is the argument for writing the negative. Why a
copy-forward entry is more dangerous than a missing one. Why escalation is the thing most worth
recording. What the local rule is for correcting an entry and for retrospective entries. And what
the reader's own regulator requires, because that is the document that actually binds.

## Scope and safety

This explains what the clinical record is for and what follows for how it is written, for someone
already training in or qualified for clinical practice. It is not a documentation standard, not a
template and not legal advice, and it deliberately states no rule about retrospective entries,
corrections, abbreviations or countersigning — all of which are set by the reader's regulator and
institution, which are the authority. Nothing here has had clinical or legal review. Nothing here
is for use in an emergency or for a decision about any person's care, and nothing in it should be
relied on in a complaint, an investigation or any legal process. If someone is unwell right now,
the local emergency number is the correct response.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The argument is structural and does not date: a record read by somebody who was not there, and a
blank that cannot distinguish "not asked" from "asked and negative", are properties of records
rather than of any era's policy. What dates is everything procedural and legal — regulator
standards, prohibited abbreviation lists, retrospective entry rules and correction mechanics —
and, faster than any of it, the technology layer. Patient access to records, automated and ambient
documentation, and the handling of machine-generated entries are all moving quickly and in
different directions in different countries, and the failure modes of each are not yet as well
characterised as copy-forward is. The reader's regulator and institution are the authority
throughout.
