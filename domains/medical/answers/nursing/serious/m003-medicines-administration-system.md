# Carefulness is not a defence. It is the thing that fails last.

Medicines administration is taught as a short list of checks — classically the right patient, the
right medicine, the right dose, the right route and the right time, with most modern teaching
adding the right documentation, the patient's right to decline, and in some countries the right
indication and the right monitoring of the response. *The core five are consensus; the number of
"rights" genuinely varies — five, seven, ten depending on the country and the textbook — so a
reader should work from their own curriculum and their own institution's policy rather than from
any single list.* Verifying allergy status before administration sits alongside them and is
consensus across major guidance.

The list looks like a prompt to be careful. It is better understood as **an index of the error
types that actually happen**, each with its own defence, because every entry corresponds to a
class of harm:

```
   the check              the error it exists to catch         who is wrong when it fails
   ─────────────────────  ───────────────────────────────────  ──────────────────────────
   right patient          the medicine reaches the wrong        usually the system: two
                          person entirely                      similar names, a moved bed
   right medicine         a look-alike or sound-alike name,     usually the system: the
                          or the wrong one of two on the        packaging and the shelf
                          same shelf                           layout
   right dose             a decimal point, a unit, a           usually the system: an
                          weight-based calculation, a          abbreviation, a
                          concentration misread                non-standard concentration
   right route            the right drug into the wrong         usually the system:
                          line or cavity — often the most       unlabelled lines, a
                          serious class of all                 connector that fits
   right time             too early, too late, or the           usually the system: the
                          omission that nobody records as an   round is longer than the
                          error at all                         time allowed for it
   right documentation    the next person cannot tell           usually the system: the
                          whether it was given                 chart design
```

## Why diligence is the wrong frame

The frame matters because it determines what gets fixed. If an error is a failure of care, the
response is to tell people to be more careful, and *the human-factors literature is consistent
that this does not reduce error rates.* If an error is the predictable output of a system, the
response is to change the system so the error becomes hard to make, and that does.

The model usually taught is layered defences — the "Swiss cheese" picture, in which harm requires
a hole to line up in every layer at once:

```
   ┌──────── layers that exist BEFORE anyone picks up a syringe ─────────────────┐
   │ electronic prescribing with decision support                                │
   │ standard concentrations and ready-to-administer preparations                │
   │ hazardous items removed from general ward stock                             │
   │ look-alike / sound-alike names separated, and distinguished in print        │
   │ prohibited abbreviations; doses written in full                             │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   ┌──────── layers AT the point of administration ──────────────────────────────┐
   │ patient identity confirmed against the chart                                │
   │ allergy status confirmed                                                    │
   │ the label read at the moment of administration, not from memory             │
   │ barcode or electronic verification, where it exists                         │
   │ lines and routes labelled; connectors chosen so wrong-route is harder       │
   │ independent second check for defined high-risk categories                   │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   ┌──────── layers AFTER ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ documentation at the time; monitoring of the expected response              │
   │ incident reporting, which is the only way a latent hole becomes visible     │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   An individual's carefulness is ONE layer. It is the thinnest, the most variable,
   and the one that degrades with fatigue, noise and workload — which is exactly the
   argument for not relying on it.
```

*Which defences are in place is highly variable.* Electronic prescribing, barcode administration,
which medicine categories require a second checker, and whether a second checker is required at
all, differ between countries and between institutions in the same country. The principle —
layered, independent defences rather than one vigilant person — is the part that generalises.

## Where the system is weakest

**Interruptions.** *Interruption during the administration round is one of the most consistently
identified contributors in the research literature,* and it is the one most amenable to design:
protected rounds, visible signals, and a culture in which interrupting is itself treated as a
safety event.

**Transitions of care.** Admission, transfer between wards, transfer between institutions, and
discharge are where discrepancies cluster — omissions, duplications, continued medicines that
should have stopped, stopped medicines that should have continued. Medicines reconciliation at
each transition is the named defence, *and that it is required at transitions is consensus; who
performs it and within what time is local.*

**A double-check that is not independent.** Two people checking together, with the second watching
the first and agreeing, detects far less than two people checking separately — and it can make
things worse, because responsibility is diffused and each believes the other is the real check.
*This is a well-described finding in the safety literature rather than a guideline claim, and it
is the single most useful thing to understand about second checking.*

**Alert fatigue and overrides.** A decision-support system that fires on everything trains people
to dismiss everything, including the alert that mattered. Override rates on automated cabinets and
electronic charts are a measure of how much a defence has stopped being a defence.

**Verbal and telephone orders.** Unavoidable sometimes; a known weak point always; and the defence
is read-back plus prompt written confirmation.

**Infusions.** The harm of a rate or programming error is larger than the equivalent oral error
because the dose is delivered over time and cannot be taken back. Smart pumps with dose limits are
a defence where they exist; a pump programmed outside its library is one that is not.

**Workarounds, which are locally rational.** The barcode that will not scan and gets taped to the
trolley, the chart signed at the start of the round because the round is longer than the time
allowed. Every workaround is a true report about a defence that does not fit the work, and
treating it as a discipline problem removes the report without fixing the mismatch.

**Unfamiliarity.** A different ward, a different country's conventions, an agency shift, a night.
The defences that depend on knowing where things are kept all weaken at once.

**Under-reporting.** A latent hole that nobody reports stays open. An error reported is a defect
found, and a system that punishes the report keeps the defect.

## The human stakes, said plainly

Two things are true at once, and holding both is the point of this topic.

A medicines error can seriously harm a patient, and sometimes does. That is why the apparatus
exists, and no amount of systems language should soften it.

And the person who makes one is, almost always, a competent professional working inside a system
that made that error easy — short-staffed, interrupted, with two boxes that look alike. The
distress afterwards is severe and well recognised, and institutions that treat an error as a
character failure lose both the person and the information. A just culture is not leniency. It is
the only arrangement under which the system finds out what is wrong with it.

## What an examiner digs into next

Which "rights" the local policy names, and what the local list adds to the classic five. What a
genuinely independent second check looks like, and which categories of medicine require one where
they work. How medicines reconciliation is performed at admission and discharge and by whom. Why
wrong-route errors are treated as a separate and more serious class, and what design features
prevent them. And what happens locally when an error is reported — because that answer predicts
whether the next one gets reported.

## Scope and safety

This explains why a safety system is built the way it is, for someone already training in or
qualified for clinical practice. It is not a protocol, not a decision aid, and it contains no
doses, no concentrations and no product names, deliberately. Every product-specific question
belongs to the formulary and the local policy, which are the authority; this is not, and it has
had no clinical review. Nothing here is for use in an emergency or for a decision about any
person's care — and nothing here should be read as describing what any individual's treatment
ought to be. If someone is unwell right now, the local emergency number is the correct response.

## Where this stands, October 2026

The reasoning is stable and has been for decades: harm comes from aligned holes in multiple
layers, and the thinnest layer is an individual's attention. What dates is the technology and the
policy — which defences are electronic, how decision support is tuned, which medicine categories
require a second checker, what counts as a prohibited abbreviation, and what the local list of
"rights" contains. All of those are reissued, and the reader's own policy and formulary are the
authority for every one of them.
