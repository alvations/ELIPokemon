# Standing sources — endocrinology

The documents a reader should open to check claims in this specialty. **None of them has been
retrieved**; this is a list of where to look, not a record of what was read. See
[`../SAFETY.md`](../SAFETY.md#sourcing-and-why-there-are-no-quotations) for why.

An answer's own `## Sources` section should **point at this file** for the standing documents and
list only what is specific to that answer. Repeating a near-identical 180-word block a hundred
times is waste, and it drags the scorer's density measure down for no reader benefit.

## The standing list

* **Your national formulary**, for anything about a medicine — dose, interaction, monitoring,
  licensed indication, formulation. It is the authority and this directory is not.
* **Your national body for clinical guidelines**, for thresholds, intervals, referral criteria and
  care pathways. These differ by country and are revised on a cycle.
* **Your own institution's policy**, for anything procedural. It overrides a national guideline
  where the two differ, and it is what you will be held to.
* **A current standard textbook of the specialty**, for definitional and mechanistic material —
  the vocabulary, the physiology, the classification systems.
* **The primary literature**, for any claim this directory marks as contested or as resting on
  audit or observational evidence rather than on mechanism.

## How to use it

Every claim in this specialty is marked inline with its basis: *mechanism*, *definitional*,
*consensus*, or *country-dependent*. A claim marked *mechanism* is checkable by reasoning and needs
no document. A claim marked *country-dependent* needs **yours**, not the one a writer had in mind.
